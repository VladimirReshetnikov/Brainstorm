## Assessment

These are compatible variants of an object-oriented mathematical workbench, not three fundamentally different logics. Their strongest common increment over round 1 is that computation must return a specified relationship to a retained mathematical object: equality, equality on a domain, a derivative-existence statement, finite coefficient agreement, solution-set coverage, or numerical enclosure. Guard scopes, observation limits, and the actual execution used to establish that relationship belong in the interface.

The most useful division of contributions is:

- **Noema:** a compact relation-and-guard calculus, disciplined conditional results, explicit distinction between construction and noncomputable selection, and the best small executable model of a request-bound cancellation certificate. Its sufficient-not-minimal guard policy is particularly sensible.
- **Prism:** the strongest parameterized symbolic-calculus example, using a Lambert-type derivative-polynomial recurrence; the most developed backward precision rules; and an explicit ergonomic correction allowing a user to delegate witness construction by specification without approving every formula.
- **Vantage:** the clearest separation of exact presentations, restricted observations, and directed abstractions; the strongest new definition-interface example, normalized EGF coefficients through Touchard/Bell/Spivey; and a decisive distinction between calculus nonderivability and object-level negation.

All three state clearly that their proposed compiler, Lean contracts, CAS checkers, and integration tests are unimplemented. Their Python companions are reproducible finite exact-arithmetic models. None supplies a Lean-verified CAS kernel, frontend fidelity theorem, end-to-end Leant protocol, user study, or measured compression/maintenance improvement. The convergence is valuable design evidence, but all three read the same two syntheses at the same revision and overlap heavily in source examples. It is not independent validation of usability or demand.

## Noema in detail

### Organizing abstraction and decisions

Noema has three foundational author requests: establish a claim, transform under a specified relation, and introduce an object. `obtain`, `construct`, `define`, and noncomputable `select` have deliberately different logical effects (§3.1, N155–173). An enclosure is a relation-bearing result rather than a fourth logical primitive. Global equality is the default for expression transformation; weaker domain/precision contracts must be explicit (N159–169).

Its relation table refuses a universal coercion lattice (§4.1, N207–222). Equality on two sets composes on their intersection, but a requested domain may not shrink silently (§4.2, N224–238). Error transport needs triangle/Lipschitz theorems and appropriate domains; downward rounding changes the claim. This is a good specification for mixed symbolic/numeric documents without pretending every relation is equality.

Finite precision is exact coefficient agreement below `N`, distinct from analytic big-O (§4.3, N240–260). Addition/product preserve a common precision; differentiation requires one more input coefficient; agreement for every `N` yields full series equality. The negative example is `t` versus `t+t^3`: agreement below order 3 becomes derivative agreement only below order 2. Demand propagation is proposed, not implemented (§4.4, N262–265).

The guard calculus is unusually careful about sufficiency (§5.4, N318–331): different methods can need different sufficient guards, so method failure is not theorem refutation. No weakest-precondition promise is made. Guard sharing requires actual equality/equivalence in context; circular use of a conclusion to establish its own premise is prohibited (§5.2, N289–294). Dependent stages bind the actual intermediate value before forming later obligations (§5.3, N296–316).

Definition templates generate ordinary definitions and proof obligations, not assumed laws. The Abel package keeps an arbitrary solution, a canonical construction, and its finite jet distinct (§§7.1–7.2, N401–424). Route selection is conservative: pin a route initially; automatically change routes only within an explicitly certified coherent family (§7.4, N433–452). Stable semantic role IDs attach to project-owned wrappers, whose mapping is checked after upstream refactoring (§7.5, N454–459).

### Six mathematical cases and neighbors

1. **Binomial kernel and inversion** (§9.1, N539–574): split `n<j`; otherwise `n=j+m`, use `k=j+i`, binomial product, alternating row. Integer coefficients then act on any additive commutative group. No multiplication on the value group or field structure is needed. Invalid neighbors: missing upper endpoint, noninjective map without multiplicity contract, changing the group to a field.
2. **Autonomous derivatives** (§9.2, N576–618): retain an induction identity on the entire open set and derive neighborhood equality at the current point; require derivative information only on the reached range. Specialize to `W'=W²`, `G_n(w)=n!w^(n+1)`, yielding `D^nW=n!W^(n+1)`. The arbitrary-index result needs parameterized derivative/factorial theorems, not sampled derivatives. Pointwise equality at zero of `t` and `0` is the negative neighbor. For an implementation, state `W`'s derivative hypothesis with actual derivative existence; Prism and Vantage make this explicit in their matching theorem statements, whereas Noema's displayed `W'=φ(W)` relies more on conventional mathematical notation (N579–586).
3. **Abel coefficients** (§9.3, N620–656): for arbitrary `T=t exp(-aT)` over any commutative rational algebra, Lagrange–Bürmann yields `[t^n]exp(xT)=x(x-na)^(n-1)/n!` for `n≥1`, with constant coefficient handled separately. Scalar inverses are transported from Q, valid even in the trivial ring; no scalar-map injectivity or field assumption. Preserve arbitrary `T`; a canonical inverse is not an implicit replacement. Separate finite/universal and formal/analytic boundaries.
4. **Floor-square-root sum** (§9.4, N658–682): the familiar first-round double count produces `S+Q=s(n+1)` and `6Q=s(s+1)(2s+1)`. These balance equations certify both natural subtraction and exact division. It is explicitly credited as an existing alternative plan, not a new view or newly discovered proof. Neighbors: `(2-3:Nat)` and `(3/2:Nat)` transported incorrectly.
5. **Cubic root enclosure** (§9.5, N684–713): derivative lower-bound certificate `3x²-1=2+3(x-1)(x+1)≥2` on `[1,2]`, IVT and uniqueness, then exact signs at `331/250` and `53/40`. Width is `1/1000`; midpoint error `<1/2000`. Root selection is explicitly noncomputable choice in the opening session (N75–103), separate from constructing executable algebraic-number data. One interval is not a global root enumeration; strict endpoint signs cannot be replaced by zero under the same contract.
6. **Radical denesting** (§9.6, N715–730): `sqrt(3+2sqrt2)=1+sqrt2` via square identity and nonnegative branch. Negative branch passes the polynomial equation but fails the selection condition. Real, complex-principal and algebraic-extension interpretations require different contracts.

### Actual executable evidence

`certificate_demo.py` is a small exact-rational univariate polynomial model. `Request` binds four numerator/denominator polynomials, scope and revision (lines 83–104); its origin digest includes the hard-coded Q conditional-equality relation. `guards()` normalizes denominator polynomials to monic form, removes known nonzero constants, and deduplicates proportional guards. `check` recomputes the original residual and expected guards (129–138); a producer does not supply proof authority.

It exercises wrong origin, sibling scope, stale revision, altered target, forged residual, omitted/substituted guard, zero denominator unsupported, and positive/unconditional cases (168–236). The exact arithmetic does not validate an actual dependent telescope, Lean environment identity, universal analytic theorem, or transport of a live source edit. Producer and checker share polynomial operations; the experiment is useful boundary modeling, not an independently verified implementation or a benchmark for compact certificates. The residual is recomputed rather than checked through an optimized independent certificate algorithm.

Fresh run: **25 unit tests, zero errors/failures**, including **182 binomial instances inside one test**. The reproduced JSON matches the delivered result except Python version (`3.14.4` versus `3.13.5`); source digest matches. It would be misleading to compare “25” directly against the other reports' thousands of per-instance arithmetic checks.

## Prism in detail

### Organizing abstraction and decisions

Prism's central result is a dependent pair of output and specified claim (§4.1, P235–246). A derivative output means actual `HasDerivAt`/within-domain evidence; complete solving means a biconditional defining the whole solution set (§4.2, P248–264). If output data depends on unresolved witnesses, the result is a family, not an already accepted object (§4.3, P280–284).

The representation relation `Rep(s,r)` need not be functional, bijective, or injective (§5.1, P292–299). Exact observations have an implementation and theorem `Rep(s,r) → o(s)=ô(r)`; two routes then agree at that observation (§5.3, P311–326). This is not equality of interval endpoints or finite arrays. Its deliberately smaller initial replacement promise keeps the semantic witness fixed and replaces only a certified representation (§5.4, P328–331). Representation-dependent clients must be invalidated when the representation changes.

Prism most explicitly resolves the choice/ergonomics question (§13.4, P832–841): `construct delta satisfying 0<delta, delta<a, delta<b` delegates the formula to the provider; `min(a/2,b/2)` may be accepted against that specification without a redundant confirmation dialog. Once accepted, the witness is fixed. This is compatible with strict semantic fidelity if delegation is represented as a permission on the construction request, rather than inferred from successful search.

Its logical/operational separation uses a stable `Reindex.v1` wrapper schema with semantic role names, mapping to the upstream theorem checked at package build (§13.1, P811–816). Three implementation families suffice: theorem adapters, computational transformations, and structural eliminators (§13.2, P818–825). Symbolic exponents indexed by arbitrary `n` must use parameterized theorems instead of pretending an array-based polynomial normalizer can expand unknown size (§13.3, P827–830).

### Worked mathematics and precision

- **Binomial inversion** (§8, P452–494) matches Noema's integer kernel/additive-group argument. The explicit negative example is a half-open source interval omitting the endpoint. Proving the final theorem another way must not retain the false advertised reindexing explanation (P488–491).
- **Lambert derivative family** (§9, P496–604) is the major distinctive worked example. With `φ(w)=exp(-w)/(1+w)`, `P₁=1`, and `P_(n+1)=(1+w)P'_n-(nw+3n-1)P_n`, define `G_n=exp(-nw)P_n/(1+w)^(2n-1)` for `n≥1`. Product/quotient differentiation under `w≠-1` gives `G'_n φ=G_(n+1)`; the autonomous theorem supplies the arbitrary-order result. A separate paper induction proves degree `n-1` and leading coefficient `(-1)^(n-1)(n-1)!` (§9.4, P582–596). The first four polynomials and denominator exponents are explicit. It applies to any locally specified solution, not a silently selected global Lambert branch. The base `n=0` case is separate (P553–558).
- **EGF coordinate equivalence and Abel identity** (§10, P606–704): `E_A(a)_n=ρ(1/n!)a_n`, inverse `D_A(F)_n=ρ(n!)[t^n]F`, multiplication corresponds to binomial convolution. The paper proof uses forward scalar identities and works over a Q-algebra with zero divisors (§10.1, P607–638). Apply Lagrange to arbitrary `T`; EGF product/injectivity yields the Abel binomial identity (§§10.3–10.4, P655–698). Finite outer polynomial or other admissible substitutions can exceed the initial zero-constant fragment; unsupported sufficient premise is not mathematical impossibility (§10.5, P700–703).
- **Precision compiler** (§11, P705–758): besides addition/product and differentiation, multiplication by `t^s` raises precision by `s`; inner replacement preserves `N` under zero-constant conditions; outer agreement to `K` and inner order at least `v≥1` give agreement to `Kv`. Backward demands include derivative `N+1`, integration `max(N-1,0)` plus a selected constant, and composition outer order `ceil(N/v)` with inner order `N` (§11.3, P734–752). These are sufficient bounds, valid as lower-order statements even with zero divisors; not promises of optimal precision. A requested coefficient `n` requires precision at least `n+1`.
- **Roots and numerics** (§12, P759–808): denest `sqrt(5-2sqrt6)=sqrt3-sqrt2` with branch evidence; cubic bracket at `1324717/10^6` and `1324718/10^6` gives midpoint error `<1/(2·10^6)`. This sharper bracket does not mean Prism's language is more capable than Noema; they chose different exact arithmetic examples. Antiderivative correctness does not automatically prove definite integration across singularities or fix constants on disconnected components (P800–803).

### Actual executable evidence

`experiments/exact_experiments.py` uses exact Q coefficients and dual numbers. Candidate generation implements the recurrence (66–74), while `check_lambert_step` independently expands the quotient-rule cross-product identity (77–94). That is a better test than merely comparing a recurrence implementation with itself, but it checks only the rational polynomial identity for concrete indices; it does not verify exponential differentiation or nonzero guards. It generates `P₁…P₉`, checks eight steps, and rejects eight coefficient corruptions plus `n=0` (115–129).

Fresh run: **2,475 reported finite checks**. Delivered and reproduced `results.json` and `certificates.json` match exactly. Counts: 8 Lambert checks, 9 mutations, 625 integer and 768 modular binomial instances, 520 dual-number EGF product and 520 inverse-coordinate checks, 20 precision counterexamples, 2 root endpoint signs, 3 arithmetic/pole controls (P1046–1070). The dual-number algebra explicitly has a nonzero nilpotent; this guards against a field-only demonstration. The modular binomial tests do not formalize arbitrary additive-group inversion. The program's counts are manually assigned/per-loop instance counts, not independent theorem counts or coverage percentages. It rejects `-O` because assertions are tests (Python lines 210–218); fresh runs used `-B`, not optimization.

No parser, actual relation/guard object, source-origin protocol, Lean reifier, live Leant replay, or general precision planner is implemented by this companion.

## Vantage in detail

### Organizing abstraction and decisions

Vantage separates three interfaces more sharply than the other two: exact presentation of the whole semantic object, restricted observation, and directed abstraction (§1.2, V173–192; §§3.2–3.3, V369–420). Its exact core prefers a validity predicate and a functional meaning map; general relations remain available explicitly. Germs belong to a projected local object, not to the graph of exact presentations of a global function (§5.6, V749–759). CAS simplification is expressly excluded from Lean definitional equality (§3.5, V442–452).

Anchor-relative coherence is elementary but powerful (§5.1, V634–653): presentations that both denote `d` agree under any semantic observation `q`. Factor-list order is not such an observation unless made part of the object/specification. This answers an important part of the diamond question without requiring global normal forms. It does not solve route selection, dependent transport, representation leaks, or the cost of establishing the common anchor.

Four surface verbs distinguish claim, present, observe and select (§4.2, V506–537); scoped existential elimination remains separate. Required named transformations, optional `prefer`, and unrestricted search have three distinct explanation policies (§4.5, V589–604). Vantage is more explicit than Noema/Prism about preference being only a ranking hint and the renderer naming the actual accepted method.

The result algebra includes a first-class **covered result** (§7.5, V989–1015): branch conditions, proof of their coverage, and per-branch specifications. Overlapping branches require uniqueness or a deterministic output-selection policy. `ax=b` illustrates the nonzero, all-solutions, and no-solutions parameter branches (§7.6, V1017–1028). Merely checking individual roots/factors is never complete solving or irreducibility.

### Distinctive mathematics

1. **Negative outcomes and nonderivability** (§5.7, V761–787). Positive transfer requires `A→P`; negative transfer of a checked `¬A` requires `P→A`. Beyond that shared warning, complete intuitionistic proof search failing to derive `P∨¬P` cannot yield `¬(P∨¬P)`: the article writes an intuitionistic proof of `¬¬(P∨¬P)` (V773–782). This exposes a misconception that a mere “safe abstraction + complete search” badge can conceal. Distinguish three claims: the search found no proof in a calculus, a countermodel refutes validity under a specified semantics, and Lean proves a negation of the original object-level proposition. They require different bridges. No claim is made that Leant currently commits this particular error.
2. **Algebraic consequences and cancellation** (§8.1, V1051–1077). A certificate `p=Σ q_i f_i` gives ideal membership, not every semantic consequence. `d^k p=Σq_i f_i` needs cancellation for `d^k`; nonzero is insufficient in a ring with zero divisors. Unit/regularity evidence is the proper general contract. The rational-function identity in Q(X) is unconditional as a fraction-field identity, but pointwise total-real evaluation remains conditional (§8.2, V1079–1099). Continuity of the nonvanishing denominator supplies the local neighborhood for derivative transport.
3. **Denesting, direct and isolated-root routes** (§§8.3–8.4, V1101–1171). The explicit identity `(v-u-1)(v+u+1)=(v²-3-2u)-(u²-2)` plus positive second factor proves the radical identity. A second route uses `X⁴-6X²+1` and Sturm chain `P,4X³-12X,3X²-1,(32/3)X,1`, with one sign variation at 2 and zero at 3. Two expressions are roots in `(2,3)`, hence equal. Irreducibility is not needed for unique-root representation. The article explicitly limits the checker questions around repeated roots, endpoints, zero polynomials and multiplicities. It also says the simple square/sign proof should not be displaced by the complex root solver merely to showcase architecture.
4. **Normalized EGF interface** (§§9.1–9.4, V1203–1329). Define `ecoeff_n(F)=ρ(n!)[X^n]F`; prove both inverses with EGF coordinates, product binomial convolution, and derivative shift, preserving even a trivial Q-algebra. Raw sequence pointwise multiplication must not be silently replaced by convolution; use named operations or a distinct wrapper (§9.3, V1280–1307). The coefficient compiler recognizes constants, sums, scalars, products, derivatives and `exp(aX)`, with unsupported expressions left typed/explicit.
5. **Touchard → shifted Bell → Spivey** (§§9.5–9.7, V1331–1436). `T_(m+1)(Y)=Y(T'_m(Y)+T_m(Y))` follows from the Stirling recurrence including boundary coefficients. With `U=exp X` and `DB=UB`, induction yields `D^mB=T_m(U)B`. Normalized coefficient extraction gives `B_(n+m)=Σ_j≤m Σ_k≤n S(m,j) choose(n,k) B_k j^(n-k)`. Forward scalar transport into arbitrary A differs from the final reflection of the natural identity through Q's injective natural embedding. This is the best example among the three of changing a public observation API to avoid creating and cancelling the same factorial factors repeatedly. Ordinary Lean with that API may receive all the gain (§9.8, V1438–1448).
6. **Analysis and finite sums** (§10, V1462–1575): preserves range-local derivative existence; same endpoint-sensitive integer binomial proof; finite antidifference certificates; and pointwise convergence of `n·1_(0,1/n)` with integral 1 as a limit-interchange negative neighbor. The `near x` diagnostic explicitly identifies missing neighborhood equality before attempting derivative search (§10.2, V1508–1523).

**One small mathematical specification gap to fix in any unified contract:** the finite telescoping formula in §10.4 (V1544–1558) must state an interval-order condition or an empty-range branch. For an inclusive natural interval, the displayed formula `Σ_(k=a)^b f(k)=G(b+1)-G(a)` is false for arbitrary `a>b+1`, even if `f(k)=G(k+1)-G(k)` holds everywhere: take integer-valued `G(k)=k`, `f(k)=1`, `a=3`, `b=1`; the sum is 0 and the RHS is −1. The report mentions endpoint conditions generally, so this is a missing explicit premise in an illustrative contract, not evidence of an implemented unsound checker. Include `a≤b` (or the slightly broader appropriate condition) and handle empty ranges explicitly.

## Responses to round-1 questions and actual differences

Noema explicitly answers all ten plus twelve questions separately in Appendix A (N871–912); Vantage does so with IDs A1–A10 and B1–B12 (V2098–2135). Prism groups them into fifteen question families rather than pretending to count twenty-two separate answers (P1087–1111). Their answers largely agree:

- **Smallest contract:** ordinary theorem wrapper, with separate generic support for computation and structural plans. None believes all normalizers/induction mechanisms reduce to binder annotations alone.
- **Binding reasons:** required transformations versus open checkpoints; Vantage explicitly retains a third preference mode.
- **Meaning review:** resolved domains, quantifier dependencies, instances, branches and precision; notices disclose semantics and do not discharge guards.
- **Roles/refactoring:** stable project-owned role schemas, checked wrappers; neither raw positions nor upstream binder names are permanent identities.
- **Definitions:** narrow ordinary-Lean packages and templates first; prove interface obligations. No general English definition compiler.
- **Publication:** accepted witness/specification and term/certificate evidence; rejected alternatives optional; all formal assertions checked. Tactic text is not automatically search-free replay.
- **Providers:** Leant remains optional Haskell-backed candidate generation behind Lean-owned identities. Measure structural composition separately from ordinary single-lemma retrieval. Negative evidence needs actual logical direction/adequacy, not a callback label.
- **Evaluation:** same-library Lean comparison, held-out modules, target/alias/cache leakage exclusions, infrastructure creation and repair charged, author/reader/maintenance measures, library/renderer fallback if syntax provides no incremental value.

## Misconceptions to exclude from the unified report

1. **Nonderivability is not negation.** All three demand proved negative transfer, and Vantage supplies the strongest object-level counterexample. A complete calculus decision can prove absence of derivations or refute universal validity without proving the negation of a fixed opaque Lean proposition. Retain the exact judgment, semantics and translation directions.
2. **A verified source algorithm does not certify an arbitrary native result.** All three explicitly require justified evaluation, execution equality, verified traces, or checked certificates. Reification/interpretation is equally necessary. This is a core common commitment, not a Vantage-only innovation.
3. **A tested Python checker is not a verified CAS implementation.** The finite companions catch arithmetic and boundary mutations. They do not implement the complete language or close the Lean trust boundary. Test counts differ in unit and should remain separate.
4. **Exact presentation and partial information are not interchangeable.** Two intervals can enclose the same real without being equal; two jets can agree below a bound without representing the entire series exactly; two germs can agree without global equality.
5. **A sufficient guard is not necessarily necessary.** Failure of a selected method or route does not refute the target. Conversely, a successful unrelated proof cannot validate an invalid advertised map or method.
6. **The scalar map need not be injective for EGF identities.** Rational unit equations transport forward into arbitrary Q-algebras, including the trivial ring. Reflection back to naturals through Q is a separate particular theorem. A nonzero zero divisor is not cancellable.
7. **Similar proposals are not independent empirical results.** Shared references, questions and examples explain much of the convergence. All three already acknowledge no authoring/usability evidence; the synthesis should preserve that boundary.

## Discriminating experiments for the next iteration

1. **Common request schema, three adversarial payloads.** Port the Noema request/guard boundary to a small Lean interface; consume one polynomial certificate, one derivative-family step, and one normalized EGF coefficient. Freeze exact input, coefficient structure, relation, guard telescope and output. Mutate each independently, including a provider supplying a correct certificate for the wrong original expression. Require an original-target proof, not only a successful Boolean callback.
2. **A factorial normalization ablation with genuine reuse.** Build normalized EGF observations once; compare ordinary-coefficient Lean, normalized-coefficient Lean, and a structured frontend over the identical package on Abel and Bell/Spivey. Hold out one family while developing on the other. Charge inverse/product/derivative laws, wrapper maintenance and any per-theorem helpers. This directly tests whether the new abstraction, rather than syntax, removes work.
3. **Lambert universal-versus-finite boundary.** Use the existing concrete `P₁…P₉` evidence as regression data, then formalize the parameterized derivative step and degree theorem. The frontend must reject an array of eight verified instances as an answer to `∀n`. Track whether handling symbolic exponents requires bespoke machinery beyond ordinary theorem wrappers.
4. **Precision composition under nilpotent substitution.** Compare Prism's zero-constant/order rules with a series whose constant coefficient is a nonzero nilpotent. Preserve formal substitution when admissible but reject use of the wrong simple precision rule. Test derivative, composition, changed requested order and cache reuse; every returned observation must carry the actually established bound. This distinguishes a relation-aware planner from a truncating CAS wrapper.
5. **Delegated witness versus accepted object.** Construct a radius under a fixed spec automatically; then add a client observing its exact value. Compare initial delegation, scoped existential elimination, named retention and later replacement. Measure unnecessary interruptions as well as semantic errors. This resolves the main ergonomic difference among the three.
6. **Negative-result semantics without a safety-bit shortcut.** Use an opaque propositional atom and excluded middle in an intuitionistic fragment; ensure nondeducibility is not promoted to negation. Separately provide a checked abstract negation plus a `P→A` bridge and require the composed original-target proof. Include bounded exhaustion, actual counterexample, rejected method guard, and successful alternative proof as different outputs.
7. **One complete algebra query and nearby weaker answers.** Ask to solve `ax=b`, not just find `b/a`. Omit singular branches, return one root as a complete list, or strip a branch selection. Test `Covered` only when completeness is requested; avoid imposing global coverage on an explicitly one-witness query.
8. **Route/observation leak after package composition.** Two independently authored packages return differently ordered factor lists or different interval enclosures. Semantic polynomial observations should agree; list-order clients require visible invalidation; interval intersection may refine containment without equality. Use the guarded Nat→Int→Q route and a nonreflecting mod-two alternative as the negative case.
9. **Replay cost versus producer cost.** Remove all discovery providers and replay the same retained artifacts. Separately time reification, generation, exact checking and source rendering. Compare a compact DAG/trace with expanded polynomial evidence. No claim that certificate checking is cheap until measured.
10. **Finite telescoping endpoint contract.** Add the omitted empty-range neighbor identified above, plus denominator support at `b+1`. It is a small example where the new language should expose the exact missing contract rather than treating “telescoping” as sufficient explanation.
