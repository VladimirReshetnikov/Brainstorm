## The frontier increment: precise, useful, and graph-relative

Fix a finite set of grounded atoms, fixed known facts K, designated additional premises H, and a finite set R of Horn implications. The target frontier is

`F(q) = Min_{⊆} { S ⊆ H | q belongs to closure_R(K ∪ S) }`.

A member S is a sufficient collection of **additional** premises for one registered graph. An empty support means already derivable; an empty family means no allowed addition suffices **in that graph**. Neither an empty family nor budget exhaustion proves negation. Ground atoms can still mention arbitrary fixed local mathematical variables; “ground” means that their data arguments are fixed, not that all real values have been enumerated. Fennel `Fennel.tex:500–638`; Juniper `Juniper.tex:324–430`.

Both engines propagate antichains by unioning premise supports and removing dominated supersets. The antichain itself is not monotone under ordinary inclusion. Its upward closure is: each accepted insertion adds an atom/support pair never previously covered, giving at most `|A| 2^|H|` accepted insertions for a fixed graph. A fair complete run yields the exact inclusion-minimal antichain, independently of schedule, although retained proof DAGs differ. Parent nodes always precede children, so cyclic *rules* do not create circular *proofs*. Historical nodes remain retained after antichain domination because later proofs may refer to them. These are written finite-calculus arguments, not proofs of the Python programs. Fennel `Fennel.tex:531–603`, `prototype/fennel.py:157–203`; Juniper `Juniper.tex:341–399`, `companion/juniper_frontier.py:123–165`.

Juniper advances the checker boundary. Its independent checker requires: (1) every stored support has a valid derivation, (2) all known/offered seeds are covered, and (3) every tuple of current premise supports for every rule is covered by a conclusion support contained in their union. Soundness plus these coverage conditions gives exact upward closure; the antichain representation then gives the exact frontier. The checker does not call the producer. `Juniper.tex:401–418`; `companion/juniper_frontier.py:168–225`. Fennel's checker validates one reachable proof DAG against the caller's original program, selected query, and permitted-rule policy; it has **no input representing a claimed complete family**. Its `Plan.complete` comes from the planner. This is a difference in guaranteed output, not a found false proof. `Fennel/prototype/fennel.py:206–280`.

The fresh audit deleted one Juniper alternative and marked the result complete: rejected with `complete frontier is not rule-closed`. The same valid subset explicitly marked incomplete was accepted. A truncated result falsely marked complete was also rejected. This is the right asymmetry: route validity survives incomplete enumeration; coverage and global minimality do not. A sound incomplete antichain can contain a support that a later smaller route dominates.

There is a genuine modeling difference. Fennel permits `ask first : P` and `ask second : P`, retaining `{first}` and `{second}` as distinct roots. Juniper offers propositions directly and refuses duplicate `offer P P`. Named origins can be useful for provenance, author actions, and repair, although the underlying proofs of the same Lean proposition are proof irrelevant. Do not report these different inventories as an algorithmic disagreement. The audit restricted comparison to one pending Fennel root per offered atom and disjoint known/offered sets: **128 profiles, 768 atom frontiers agreed**, with all 128 Juniper results and 89 selected Fennel route certificates checked. Fixed seed, profile data, bounds, and hashes are in `audit.json` and `adversarial/cross-profiles.json`. This is bounded implementation agreement, not a general equivalence theorem.

Fennel also implements `using only` before search and rechecks the rule policy for every certificate node, including unused serialized nodes. It can expose a cached cast route `{cached}` alongside `{odd,residue}`, then restrict the result to the arithmetic route. `Fennel.tex:802–818,1010–1030`; `prototype/quotients.fnl:1`; `prototype/fennel.py:183,227–280`. This guarantees use of an allowed finite recipe vocabulary, not that a proof “essentially uses” a human mathematical idea or that a wrapper theorem lacks unwanted transitive dependencies.

Both reports face exponential output. Fennel's paired-choice stress run produced 4,16,64,256,1024 minimal supports for 2,4,6,8,10 pairs; the 128-node cap at ten pairs retained 108 routes and correctly reported incomplete closure. Its node cap counts retained historical nodes, not just displayed supports. ## Heather: complete affine equality on an admitted image

Heather's written theorem is sound and worth explaining self-contained. Let S be a **nonempty type of admissible complete input configurations**, and `o : S → Q^k` a joint observation. Choose actual inputs `s0,…,sr` such that all differences `o(s)−o(s0)` lie in the rational span of `o(sj)−o(s0)`. For affine maps F and G, equality at these frame inputs is equivalent to equality at every admissible input. Subtract F−G: it vanishes at the base point and its linear part vanishes on the spanning differences. Realizability gives the converse and converts a failing frame point into an actual source witness. Coverage alone can suffice for positive transfer from abstract points; realized points additionally make failures meaningful. `Heather.tex:881–948`.

If the affine hull of `o(S)` has dimension r, a realized determining frame of r+1 points exists, and fewer points cannot determine **all affine maps**. The lower bound uses an affine functional vanishing on the smaller proposed hull but not on the full hull. This is an elementary linear-algebra existence theorem, not an algorithm for solving arbitrary admissibility or discovering its affine hull. The bound may overstate the necessary samples for a smaller restricted candidate family. `Heather.tex:1029–1062`.

The implementation admits only input lists, nil, singleton zero, append, and reverse. Structural interpretation gives `length(e)=c+Σ ai ni`, with natural coefficients. The four domains have exact fixed frames:

| Domain | Frame | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| k independent `List Nat` inputs | 0 and each ei | Equalities of all coefficients decide all admitted input lengths |
| k `List Empty` inputs | 0 only | `nil` and identity have the same length behavior; positive lengths cannot refute it |
| Two `List Nat` inputs with equal lengths | (0,0),(1,1) | `length(xs ++ ys)=length(ys ++ ys)` holds under the guard |
| k `List Nat` inputs with even lengths | 0 and each 2ei | Every failing frame witness obeys evenness |

`Heather.tex:950–1027`; `companion/heather.py:144–202`. An empty element type does **not** mean empty admissible input type: `List Empty` contains nil. If S itself is empty, universal specifications are vacuous and no base frame point exists; the proposed native API needs a separate emptiness case (`Heather.tex:988`).

One useful synthesis observation: free and even-length tuples have the **same rational affine hull**. Thus the even guard introduces no new universally true affine equalities for this grammar; it changes admissible witnesses and the source statement. For example, `0=n` fails at n=1 over free lists and at n=2 over even lists. Our additional 49 expression-pair checks agree between free and even acceptance, consistent with that mathematical argument; these samples are not its proof. The diagonal and empty domains do reduce affine dimension and can admit equalities rejected on all natural tuples.

Do not extend this criterion to inequalities: `1−n ≥ 0` holds at 0 and 1 but fails at 2. Heather itself supplies this correction at `Heather.tex:1064`. Nor does length equality imply content preservation, permutation, sorting, or stability. Arbitrary branching or unproved provider behavior is outside the grammar. The required bridge to the selected Leant candidate remains a separate theorem: its observed evaluation must equal the model of this exact AST (`Heather.tex:926,1104–1126`).

The Python checker reconstructs models and the exact fixed frame, rejects changed request digests, rejects booleans/floats masquerading as integer coefficients, and checks actual concrete list evaluation for negative results. Producer and checker nevertheless **share the parser and model code**; there is no mechanically proved denotation theorem. `companion/heather.py:209–246`; `Heather.tex:1661–1679`. The fresh audit confirms diagonal→free stale-certificate rejection, an actual free counterexample with lengths (1,0), an even-domain counterexample of length 2, rejection of a forged odd-length 1 witness, and rejection of `one()` over Empty. It retained exact certificates under `adversarial/`.

## Mathematical examples worth carrying forward

**Guarded representation changes, illustrated by q-Pochhammer.** Fennel distinguishes the unconditional derivative of `P_n(x)=∏_{j<n}(1−xq^j)` as a sum of products from the quotient presentation `−P_n(a) Σ_{j<n} q^j/(1−aq^j)`, which requires each denominator nonzero. At n=1,a=1 over R, the actual derivative is −1, while Lean's totalized zero division makes the proposed unguarded quotient value zero. n=0 is valid, and no arbitrary restriction on q is needed for the polynomial derivative. For composition, guards must be at the actual `f(x)`. `Fennel.tex:845–915`. This is a better example of useful demand-directed representations than an unspecified “simplify” request.

**Preserve locality and quantifier order.** All three explain the autonomous-derivative induction: retain equality for every x in an open U; openness yields a neighborhood; derivative congruence and the chain rule then apply at x, using auxiliary differentiability only at W(x). A total derivative-value equality alone does not establish differentiability. Fennel `Fennel.tex:921–961`; Heather `Heather.tex:1346–1400`; Juniper `Juniper.tex:165–188`. Fennel further gives a good independent a.e. quantifier counterexample: for each x, the singleton indicator in y is zero a.e., but for every y there is x=y where it equals one. Thus `∀x, a.e. y, P(x,y)` cannot become `a.e. y, ∀x, P(x,y)` for an unrestricted uncountable family. `Fennel.tex:963–984`. A fixed-measure integral consumer is distinct from point evaluation; do not add integrability assumptions to integral congruence unless the exact selected theorem needs them.

**Operation-specific Frey interfaces.** All retain the inhabited tuple (3,2,5), avoid relying on a contradictory full Fermat package, and distinguish integer exactness, homomorphic balance transport, and destination inversion. A nonzero integer may map to zero in positive characteristic; nonzero, regular, and unit are different general-ring notions. Juniper explicitly improves the first coefficient's bound from p≥4 to p≥2 when b is even, p odd, and a≡3 mod4; the second coefficient still follows from b even and p≥4 alone (`Juniper.tex:546–576`). The tuple (3,2,3) separates the two consumers. This improvement is a proved library-premise refinement, not something established by antichain search on an unchanged graph. Fennel already warns its graph supports are not globally weakest (`Fennel.tex:1010–1030`); Heather's displayed first-coefficient package is sufficient, not claimed optimal (`Heather.tex:1267–1282`).

**A new analytic/CAS split in Juniper.** Let U,V⊆R be open; f smooth on U; g continuous on V; g(V)⊆U; f(g(y))=y for y∈V; and f′ nonzero on U. Define a polynomial derivation D by `D(uj)=u(j+1)` and

`P1=1`, `P(n+1)=u1 D(Pn)−(2n−1)u2 Pn`.

Then the written proof derives `g^(n)(y)=Pn(f′(g(y)),…,f^(n)(g(y)))/f′(g(y))^(2n−1)` for n≥1. It separately supplies the local inverse derivative, polynomial interpretation under actual derivatives, quotient guards, and region-wide induction. This fills a paper-level bridge rather than promoting formal power-series coefficients to analytic theorems by matching formulas. `Juniper.tex:604–677`.

The program computes P1 through P6 with exact integer sparse arithmetic; the first four separately expected polynomials agree. Ordinary degree n−1, weighted degree 2n−2, highest index n, coefficient `(-1)^(n−1)(2n−3)!!` on `u2^(n−1)`, and coefficient −1 on `u1^(n−2)un` are written structural consequences, but only the degree/weighted-degree checks and first-four formula comparisons are exercised by the provided script. These invariants alone cannot certify all coefficients. `Juniper.tex:679–703`; `companion/inverse_polynomials.py:33–83`.

Keep its negative neighbors: x³ at zero has vanishing derivative and a nondifferentiable inverse; for f(x)=x² on R\{0}, choosing +sqrt(y) on rational positive y and −sqrt(y) on irrational y gives a right inverse with image in U and nonzero f′, yet a discontinuous chosen branch. Thus the inverse law does not supply the branch-continuity premise. `Juniper.tex:721–726`. These are written counterexamples, not executed Lean examples in this package.

**Whole constructions and actual shared witnesses.** Heather/Fennel preserve every factor's finite/free module hypotheses in the finite tensor representability example. A singleton family Q[X] prevents finite-dimensionality from following from finite indexing alone; naturality is a relation for the actual representing equivalence, not independently attached vertex properties. `Fennel.tex:1063–1099`; `Heather.tex:1422–1466`. Juniper adds quotient descent: a surjection q and fiber-constant f determine a unique factor; continuity additionally needs the quotient topology. Bare surjectivity supplies no executable preimage algorithm, while an actual quotient eliminator is another constructive interface. The same factor must occur in the factorization and continuity obligations, which motivates a dependent telescope rather than unrelated existentials. `Juniper.tex:728–763`.

## Next questions and concrete gates

1. **What should completeness certify?** Mechanize Juniper's seed/rule-coverage checker in Lean, instantiate atoms/rules with checked theorem objects, and separately report grounding coverage. Fennel's richer named-root/policy semantics should be decided before freezing that format.
2. **Which equivalence on explanations is useful?** Do two proofs of the same proposition constitute distinct author actions? Should same-support proofs with different method policies be retained? Decide whether minimal support, bounded top-k display, and cheapest proof are separate outputs. All-routes output is intrinsically exponential.
3. **Can a single native list theorem close the behavioral gap?** Formalize the supported AST interpretation and registered realized frames, then link one exact Leant candidate/provider graph to that AST. Include List Empty, diagonal correlation, and free/even same-hull controls. Compile the theorem for the original selected candidate and a concrete refutation for the negative neighbor.
4. **Can publication be bound to the current request?** Use a manifest enumerating current proof artifacts, hashes, exact statements, and statuses; reject stale positive files in reused directories. The lane's two-step reproductions are ready-made regression fixtures. This tests an actual gap that a successful Lean compilation of an old file cannot detect.
5. **What is the minimal honest use site?** Resolve one exact-quotient consumer's designated proof arguments using real registered Lean theorems. Retain operation-specific premises, the p≥2 first-coefficient improvement, and destination-unit evidence. Compare with ordinary automatic parameters and an explicit service call supplied the same theorem library.
6. **Can the analytic/CAS split be mechanized without changing the statement?** Prove Juniper's differential-polynomial interpretation once; specialize P4/P6 with a checked polynomial certificate; keep the actual inverse branch, continuity, open regions, and nonzero derivative. Negative neighbors must fail at their particular guard, not be inferred false merely from a tactic failure.
7. **What should survive an edit?** Start with snapshot rebuilding; only optimize evidence-availability changes once route dependencies are retained. Deleting a rule may remove the sole retained derivation even if another discarded same-support proof exists; recomputation or multiple support proofs is required (`Fennel.tex:779–797`). Measure rebuild time before implementing persistent context migration.
8. **Which benefit survives a matched control?** Freeze registry/services and choose genuinely new authoring and reading tasks. Charge for provider proofs, coverage lemmas, wrappers, reification, and maintenance. A smaller source file obtained by a custom theorem or a more capable solver belongs to those improvements, not automatically to syntax.

The strongest next synthesis should preserve the distinction between a **sound route**, a **complete finite frontier**, an **adequate behavior model on a domain**, a **proof of the rendered original statement**, and a **readable faithful document**. The three packages supply complementary partial implementations of these obligations. Combining their claims without their boundaries would erase their most useful result.
