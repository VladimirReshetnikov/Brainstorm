\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{The task and the material}\label{sec:material}
The fourth round of this brainstorm asked nine AI-authored reports to advance a proof language over Lean~4 for working mathematicians. The brief, as every report restates it, had the same five parts as before: extend the type system so that mathematical properties and program behaviour become part of an object's type; keep the hybrid architecture in which a certified computer-algebra service and the Leant synthesis engine supply evidence; ground the design in the ProveIt corpus and the Fermat's Last Theorem repository; respond to the twenty questions the two round-3 syntheses left open; and ship a companion that executes something. All nine were written against the same immutable inputs: the two round-3 syntheses at Brainstorm commit \code{bf333390}, ProveIt at \code{24ce8bd7}, the Fermat repository at \code{aa2d8b34}, and Leant at \code{3a40904b}. \subsection{Method of this review}
Every report was read in full from its \LaTeX{} source, not from its PDF or README, together with its evidence files and Lean exports. Three kinds of artefact were then produced. First, the tables of Sections~\ref{sec:convergence}, \ref{sec:questions} and~\ref{sec:suite} are generated from one data file (\code{tools/make_tables.py}), so that every count in the text can be regenerated. Second, every shipped Lean file was compiled at the ProveIt pin, and every companion was rerun in a scratch copy under Python~3.14.4; Section~\ref{sec:toolchain} reports both. Third, two Lean files were written and kernel-checked here to test the one thing no report could test without a toolchain: whether the generic routes the companions export can be instantiated with real theorems (Section~\ref{sec:routes}) and whether the observation frames six reports prove on paper hold in core Lean (Section~\ref{sec:frames-lean}).

The review takes the reports at their word about what they inspected and ran, and checks what can be checked. Where a report's mathematics is quoted, it was reread rather than trusted. Where a report's claim about a repository could be verified locally (the Leant revision question in Section~\ref{sec:leant}), it was.

\subsection{Summary of findings}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The round converged on one object and two theorems.} All nine reports define the unfinished step as a conditional term over a residual telescope (Section~\ref{sec:unit}). Six prove the finite antichain theorem for alternative sufficient supports; the other three prove the simpler closure theorem. Five prove that a finite realizable frame decides affine equalities on the admissible observation image; \Fe{} and \Ju{} prove the two-point special case on \code{List Nat}, and \Br{} and \So{} state the image characterisation. Of 101 commitments in the matrix, 62 are unanimous, 19 of them new this round.
\item \textbf{Nineteen of the twenty shipped Lean files compile.} Twenty files, 546 lines, 37 named theorems. Eighteen compile unchanged; one needed its companion core compiled to an \code{olean} first; one of \He's five Mathlib-importing files fails as shipped because its fixed tactic template calls \code{omega} after \code{simp} has already closed the goal, and compiles once that line is removed. Every other diagnostic is an unused-variable or unused-simp-argument lint. Thirteen files are generic implications over \code{Prop} variables (twelve carry the 22 generic theorems; \La's cycle file carries none): their compilation checks proof assembly, as every report says, and nothing about the atoms' meaning. \Cl's two files and \He's five are concrete.
\item \textbf{Every companion reproduces.} All nine test suites pass here with the counts the reports state, from \He's 18 methods to \La's 98,304 exhaustively compared frontiers. One demo script fails under a Windows default code page and passes in UTF-8 mode.
\item \textbf{The missing bridge is short.} Instantiating \La's, \Br's and \So's exported routes with the round-3 Frey theorems yields the actual coefficient identities in a 133-line file (Section~\ref{sec:routes}). That is the registry link every report describes and none built; it needs the round-3 arithmetic and nothing else.
\item \textbf{Four facts behind the frames hold in core Lean.} The image characterisation of \code{length}, the one-point frame on \code{List Empty}, the two-point affine calculation, and the diagonal criterion for a list and its reverse are four theorems and three examples in 62 lines with no imports (Section~\ref{sec:frames-lean}). They are not axiom-free: the parallel synthesis's axiom audit, rerun here, shows \code{propext} throughout and \code{Quot.sound} and \code{Classical.choice} where \code{simp} and \code{omega} enter.
\item \textbf{The parallel synthesis agrees on the compiler results and corrects this review's counts.} It reached the same nineteen-of-twenty result independently, found the same Heather failure, and added adversarial probes (three Clover exports that Lean rejects, a Sorrel route selection that prefers new work to an available proof, a Rowan diagnostic frontier that is not a sufficient contract) and a Fennel--Juniper cross-check on 128 profiles. Section~\ref{sec:parallel} lists what was accepted.
\item \textbf{A correction to the corpus.} The round-3 syntheses cite a Leant revision \code{823259f7} that four reports could not retrieve. It exists only on an unpushed local branch (Section~\ref{sec:leant}). The round-3 claims about it are unchanged, but the reference was not public and should not have been cited as if it were.
\item \textbf{What did not happen.} No report measured anything on a real Lean file, ran a reading test, or held out a task; all nine say so. The recommendation of Section~\ref{sec:recommendation} is therefore the same as last round's, made more specific by what compiled: one Lean slice, whose frontier engine is now specified to the level of a test suite nine engines already pass.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background: what the first three rounds established}\label{sec:background}
This section is self-contained: a reader who has not seen the earlier rounds should be able to follow the rest of the report from it.

\subsection{Rounds one and two: the certified-computation unit}
The first round produced nine blueprints for a proof language over Lean and twenty-two questions. The second round answered them with a shared unit: a computation returns a \emph{value together with a proof of a fixed specification}, and the language distinguishes result kinds (exact equality, equality under a guard, a finite jet, an enclosure, a witness, a complete solution set) that must never be silently promoted into one another. It fixed a two-lane architecture for a verified computer-algebra service (a verified algorithm, or an untrusted algorithm with a verified certificate checker), identified the \emph{execution gap} (a theorem about a checker says nothing about bytes returned by a native process; \code{native_decide} adds a per-invocation axiom), and made \emph{reification} explicit: a certificate about an encoded expression proves the original target only through a checked bridge between encoding and source. It separated preservation from reflection ($X$ lies in the ideal $(2X)$ over $\Q[X]$ but not over $\Z[X]$), stated the derivative precision rule (a jet of order $N+1$ gives a derivative jet of order $N$), the residual-to-jet rule (a residual certificate identifies a jet of the selected root only with the right constant term and a unit derivative), and the three predicates nonzero, regular, and unit.

\subsection{Round three: the object with a growing interface}
The third round asked what a property- and behaviour-aware type system over that architecture should be. Its unit was an \emph{object fixed with a growing evidence row}: a term $t:A$ is never rebound to a subtype locally; proofs of $P(t)$ accumulate in a context-indexed evidence row; weakening applies a proved implication; conjunction combines proofs about the same term; equality transports by elimination and any other relation needs its own consumer theorem. A behavioural contract on a total function is the ordinary proposition
\[
 \Beh(f;P,Q)\;:\!\iff\;\forall x,\ P(x)\to Q(x,f(x)),
\]
with contravariant preconditions, covariant postconditions, and a composition rule that retains the actual intermediate value and generates a bridge obligation. Structures (a ring, a topology, a measure) are selected as data before properties are inferred as proofs. The kernel is unchanged; the elaborator extends. Inference is demand-driven from the premises of a chosen method and is complete only for a finite grounded fragment. A registry rule is an ordinary theorem plus role metadata. Finite evidence about behaviour needs four separate contracts: positive soundness, decision completeness, negative realization, and observation coverage; on \code{List Empty} the affine length models $0$ and $n$ differ although the programs agree. The round's review compiled seven small Lean cores (49 theorems) and kernel-checked the Frey exact-division proposition: for $a,b\in\Z$ and odd $p\ge4$ with $a\equiv3\pmod 4$ and $2\mid b$, the numerators $b^p-1-a^p$ and $-a^pb^p$ are divisible by $4$ and $16$, the second needing only $2\mid b$ and $4\le p$, so the integer quotients cast to the rational ones; the fixture $(3,2,5)$ gives $-53$ and $-486$. It also recorded two corpus corrections that this round inherits: the finite tensor-family theorem assumes every factor is finite and free, and finite indexing alone does not supply that, and an affine checker over unrestricted lengths is sound but not complete on \code{List Empty}.

\subsection{Three qualifications every report inherits}
Every fourth-round report restates three qualifications from the round-3 reconciliation and builds on them. A sound finite checker proves every claim it accepts without being complete; a failing abstract observation is a source-level counterexample only after it is realized by an admissible input and transferred in the negative direction; and a proof checked only inside a contradictory package (the full Frey package, which is eventually refuted) does not test an arithmetic interface. The reports also inherit the evaluation design: arms $L_0$ (ordinary Lean) through $L_4$ (the new surface syntax), plus a read-only renderer control, with $L_3$ versus $L_2$ as the test of the property layer.

\section{The fourth-round unit: a residual telescope with a frontier}\label{sec:unit}

\subsection{Nine names for one judgment}
Every report gives the unfinished proof step a mathematical semantics, and all nine semantics are the same. A step with a fixed target $T$ in a context $\Gamma$ produces an ordered telescope $\Delta$ of remaining obligations and a term valid under it:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residual}
 \Gamma,\Delta\vdash k:T,\qquad\text{hence}\qquad \Gamma\vdash\lambda\Delta.\,k:\Pi\Delta.\,T.
\end{equation}
The telescope is dependent and ordered; in the proof-only case it is a curried implication. The conditional term never enters the evidence index as a proof of $T$; supplying a well-typed substitution for $\Delta$ discharges it; exporting the conditional theorem is an explicit statement change. Table~\ref{tab:names} lists the notation.

\subsection{The frontier: alternative sufficient supports}
Six reports (\Al, \Br, \Fe, \Ju, \La, \So) go one step further and compute \emph{all} inclusion-minimal ways to close a step within a fixed finite fragment. The fragment is the same in all six. After grounding, there is a finite set $\mathcal Q$ of propositions in one context, a set $K\subseteq\mathcal Q$ of known facts, a designated vocabulary $H\subseteq\mathcal Q$ of premises the author is willing to be asked for, and a finite set $\mathcal R$ of ground Horn rules $q_1,\ldots,q_k\Rightarrow q$, each backed by a theorem at that instantiation. Writing $\Clos_{\mathcal R}$ for forward closure, the frontier of a goal is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:frontier}
 F(q)=\Min_{\subseteq}\{S\subseteq H:\ q\in\Clos_{\mathcal R}(K\cup S)\}.
\end{equation}
The algorithm stores an antichain per proposition, seeds known facts with $\varnothing$ and vocabulary atoms with their singletons, and for each rule inserts the union of one support per premise unless a subset is already present, deleting strict supersets. Every insertion creates an immutable derivation node that refers only to older nodes. The six reports prove the same theorem.

\begin{theorem}[stated in six reports]\label{thm:antichain}
For finite $\mathcal Q,K,H,\mathcal R$, fair iteration terminates after at most $|\mathcal Q|\,2^{|H|}$ successful insertions. Every stored support has a derivation of its goal from $K$ and that support; at the fixed point the stored antichain is exactly~\eqref{eq:frontier}; and the antichains, though not the chosen derivations, are independent of the schedule.
\end{theorem}

The proofs coincide in their one nontrivial move: the stored antichain is not monotone under insertion (a smaller support replaces larger ones), so the termination measure is the \emph{upward closure} $\uparrow F(q)=\{S:\exists T\in F(q),\,T\subseteq S\}$, which every successful insertion strictly enlarges. Completeness is induction on a forward derivation from $K\cup S$; soundness is induction on node creation, which is well founded even when the rule graph is cyclic. \Ju{} adds a checkable certificate: an antichain family is the exact frontier if every support has a valid derivation, seeds are covered, and every rule combination is dominated (its Theorem~6.4, ``frontier certification''); its validator checks this when a result is marked complete, and one of its tests deletes an alternative and asserts the certificate fails.

The other three engines compute a single route. \Cl{} grounds goal-triggered instances and runs a forward closure that retains one proof node per atom; \Ro{} builds a backward cone from the goal, closes it with premise counters in linear time, and reports unresolved leaves as the frontier; \He{} proves the closure proposition and ships a two-premise resolver. All three prove schedule independence of the closed \emph{set}, not of the chosen proof.

\subsection{What the six reports say the theorem does not say}
The agreement on limits is as complete as the agreement on the theorem. A minimal support is minimal by inclusion in a vocabulary and rule set, not a weakest precondition: if $H$ contains both $g(x)>0$ and $g(x)\ne0$, both singletons survive, and \La{} argues that this is a feature. A support does not assert that its members are true or jointly satisfiable. Offering the goal as its own repair is a valid but tautological route, which \Al, \Br, \Ju{} and \La{} exclude from the default vocabulary and \Ju{} and \La{} test. The worst case is exponential: with $k$ independent facts each provable from either of two offered premises, the goal has $2^k$ incomparable supports; \Fe, \Ju{} and \La{} run that family and report widths $4,16,\ldots,1024$, and all six say a truncated run must be labelled incomplete rather than reported as ``no route''. Method policy must be applied \emph{before} minimization (or the labels indexed by policy), because an unrestricted shortcut with a smaller support would otherwise erase a permitted argument; \So{} tests this. And two completeness flags are needed, one for grounding coverage of the profile and one for frontier closure of the emitted manifest (\Ju{} names them; \Br{} calls them two layers).

\subsection{Grounding: the bound the third round asked for}
Question T8 asked what generates the finite rule set. All nine answer with the same design and, between them, seven explicit bounds (Table~\ref{tab:bounds}). A finite typed term pool is fixed from the target's subterms, the relevant local declarations, and explicitly named intermediates. A registry template is instantiated only by matching its conclusion against a demanded atom and drawing its remaining data parameters from the pool; every candidate instance is type-checked against the actual theorem before it becomes a rule; proof parameters become premise atoms. No rule may enlarge the pool during closure (the schema $P(x)\to P(f(x))$ is the standard warning); constructors may extend it only to an explicit depth, and a construction opens a new epoch with a new bound.

Every report adds the same disclaimer: the bound proves finiteness, not tractability, and completeness is relative to the rules actually materialised. \Cl{} supplies the only measurement: on one composition request its seven-rule registry generates 211 ground instances and tests 633 rule firings to retain a four-node proof, and the two-rule profile that the proof actually uses generates 16.

\subsection{Realizable frames: the completeness theorem the third round lacked}\label{sec:frames}
Question T5 asked which Leant abstractions admit a completeness theorem, and the round-3 correction was that coefficient equality is incomplete on \code{List Empty}. Five reports answer with one theorem in five dresses. Let $S$ be the admissible inputs, $o:S\to V$ an observation into a vector space, and $x_0,\ldots,x_r\in S$ \emph{realized} points whose observations cover the image:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:frame}
 \forall x\in S,\quad o(x)-o(x_0)\in\operatorname{span}\{o(x_j)-o(x_0):1\le j\le r\}.
\end{equation}
Then two affine maps agree on $o(S)$ if and only if they agree at the $r+1$ frame points, and a failing frame point is a concrete admissible counterexample. \Al{} states it with integer combinations into an abelian group (and notes that a rational-span version fails in the presence of torsion); \Cl{} and \He{} over a field, \He{} adding that an affine hull of dimension $r$ needs and suffices with $r+1$ realized points for the full affine class; \La{} with the lifted span of $(1,o(x))$; \Ro{} for a supplied finite union of linear sets $b_j+\sum_k z_kv_{j,k}$ with $z_k\in\N$, where the criterion is $\delta+\alpha\cdot b_j=0$ and $\alpha\cdot v_{j,k}=0$ and the algorithm returns the failing point $b_j$ or $b_j+v_{j,k}$ with its membership coordinates. \Br, \Fe{} and \Ju{} prove the special cases (the image of \code{length} on \code{List}~$\alpha$ is $\{n:n=0\lor\mathrm{Nonempty}\,\alpha\}$; the two-point basis on \code{List Nat}).

The four instances are shared. Independent lists over an inhabited type: base $0$, unit directions, realized by the empty list and singletons. Lists over an empty type: base $0$, no directions; the models $0$ and $n$ agree. A list and its reverse: base $(0,0)$, direction $(1,1)$; the difference $c+an_1+bn_2$ vanishes on the diagonal exactly when $c=0$ and $a+b=0$, so the ambient witness $(1,0)$ refutes nothing. Even lengths (or lengths exactly 100, or nonempty lists): a guard changes the frame without changing the grammar. \Cl, \He{} and \Ro{} also separate an empty admissible domain (vacuous, no base point) from an empty element type (one base point). Section~\ref{sec:frames-lean} checks all four instances in core Lean.

\subsection{What the unit forbids}
The nine reports agree on the same prohibitions, stated as regression cases. A residual is an elaboration effect, not a runtime effect, and a timeout never appears in $\Delta$. A conditional route is not a proof of its unconditional target; it may be indexed only at its conditional type, and it cannot be used to prove its own guard. A frontier element is not a hypothesis of the theorem; adding it is a statement change. A model witness is not a source counterexample until realized. A generic exported implication is not a theorem about integers until its atoms are bound to theorems. And ``no route in this profile'' is never ``false''.

\section{Where the reports differ}\label{sec:divergence}

\subsection{Genuine divergences}
\textbf{Antichain or route.} Six engines retain every inclusion-minimal support; \Cl, \He{} and \Ro{} retain one route and report its unresolved leaves. The disagreement is about cost, not correctness: the antichain reports accept an exponential worst case and add budgets and partial-mode flags; \Ro{} argues that a linear-time closure with a nonminimal explanation is the safer first implementation. Both sides agree that a partial frontier's routes remain sound (\La's Theorem 6.1 makes the point that soundness needs no fixed point) and that the finite algorithm should be exposed with its completeness status. The review's view is that the antichain is the right \emph{specification} and the route the right first \emph{implementation}. A route engine preserves the sufficiency of each route it returns; it does not preserve the alternative and coverage information, so an antichain engine should remain the bounded reference answer on fixtures even if the interactive service returns one checked route with an explicit incomplete-inventory status.

\textbf{What may the sealed target contain?} \Fe{} and \Ro{} adopt the strict rule that a sealed target contains no metavariables at all (proof goals live in the term, and a construction request has a fixed type such as $\{x:A\mid\mathrm{Spec}(x)\}$). \Al, \Br, \Cl, \Ju{} and \So{} classify holes by the dependency closure of the target and reject only assignments to meaning-bearing ones. The strict rule is easier to audit and rejects some harmless cases; the classification is the eventual design. They are not in conflict, and \Ro{} says so.

\textbf{Which scalar domain for the frame theorem?} \Al{} proves the frame theorem with integer combinations into an abelian group and warns that clearing denominators fails under torsion ($2g=0$ need not give $g=0$); \Cl, \He{} and \La{} prove it over $\Q$; \Ro{} over $\N$-combinations of generators in a finite union. For lengths embedded in $\Z$ or $\Q$ every version applies. The distinction matters only if the observation codomain has torsion, which no report's grammar has; \Al's warning is correct and currently moot.

\textbf{Order of the first slice.} Eight reports ship the exact-quotient use site first; \He{} ships the list-length bridge first; \Cl{} and \Ju{} first port their finite engines to Lean. Section~\ref{sec:slice} orders the slice by what compiled here.

\section{Assessment}\label{sec:assessment}

\subsection{What is strongest}
\textbf{The finite frontier theorem is mature.} Six independent proofs of Theorem~\ref{thm:antichain}, six engines that pass their own oracles' tests, a checkable completeness certificate, an explicit exponential family with measured widths, and a policy-before-minimisation rule with a test. The theorem is well specified; its composition with dependent telescopes, authorized premises, method policy, route selection and export still holds design choices, and the parallel synthesis's probes (Section~\ref{sec:parallel}) show where two of them bite.

\textbf{The frame theorem answers the round-3 correction.} The third round could only say that coefficient equality is incomplete on \code{List Empty}. This round says what completeness \emph{is}: relative to the admissible image, decided by a realizable frame, with the frame's failing point as the counterexample. Section~\ref{sec:frames-lean} shows the instances are trivial to check; \Ro's semilinear version and \He's rank bound are the parts that would carry weight in an implementation.

\textbf{The corpus reading is careful and consistent.} Nine reports read the same six source files and agree on every mathematical detail: the autonomous-derivative theorem needs its hypotheses only on $W(U)$ and its induction must keep the point quantified; the Frey helper's second numerator needs two of its four premises; the tensor theorem takes finite free factors as hypotheses and returns a naturality \emph{law}; \code{Verified} is a callback receipt. Where a report adds a source (\Br's Fubini file, \Fe's q-Pochhammer file, \Ju's inverse-derivative file, \La's and \So's regularity file, \So's Cauchy file), its reading was rechecked against the mathematics here and holds.

\textbf{The evidence discipline held.} Every report separates written proof, executed finite test, historical check attributed to the syntheses, and design. Counts are reported with units and never summed. Every Lean file says in its first line that it was not compiled. This review found few cases in which a report claimed more than it had; the parallel synthesis found two in \So{} (an overstrong sentence about partial runs and a route-selection defect in its command-line tool, Section~\ref{sec:parallel}), and this review's own first version overcounted its list theorems.

\subsection{What is weak, over-engineered, or under-specified}
\textbf{Nine proofs of one theorem.} The antichain theorem, the frame theorem, the composition proposition, the Frey proposition and the primitive-refinement rules are each proved between six and nine times. The proofs are correct and interchangeable. A reader of all nine reports learns Theorem~\ref{thm:antichain} once and rereads it five times; the campaign's marginal cost per report has become the cost of confirming that nothing new was said.

\textbf{Thirteen of the exports prove only implications.} Thirteen of the twenty files are implications between \code{Prop} variables. Their compilation was worth doing once (Section~\ref{sec:lean-files}) and checks these emitted files and nothing about the names of their atoms; a changed emitter needs its output checked again, as the parallel synthesis's name-hygiene probes show. \Cl's core and \He's Mathlib files are the exceptions and the models: the former proves seven rules about endomorphisms, the latter the length laws about actual lists, and one of them (\code{empty_preserves}) is precisely the \code{List Empty} case.

\textbf{The surfaces are still sketches.} Every report shows a proposed syntax (\code{establish}, \code{derive}, \code{know}, \code{construct}, \code{obtain}, \code{on U}, \code{keeping the point general}) and no shipped parser accepts it, though \Al, \Cl, \Fe, \Ro{} and the others do parse small fragments of their own. Eight different vocabularies for four verbs (introduce data, assume, prove, construct) is the same situation as round three's nine spellings of one judgment, one level up.

\textbf{Grounding is bounded and unmeasured.} Seven formulas bound the same enumeration (Table~\ref{tab:bounds}); \Cl's single measurement (211 instances for a four-node proof in a five-term arena) is the only datum, and it is in a Python model of endomorphisms.

\textbf{The frame theorem is elementary and the coverage proof is not.} Six reports prove that a frame decides affine laws; \La{} alone says plainly that proving \emph{coverage} of the admissible image can be harder than proving the original behavioural law, and that the approach pays only when many candidates share a domain. The four executed frames all have trivial coverage proofs. No report exhibits a frame whose coverage proof is nontrivial.

\subsection{Blind spots common to all nine}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Nobody instantiated a route.} The bridge from a generic export to a theorem is 133 lines and needs no new machinery (Section~\ref{sec:routes}). Every report describes it as the next step; none took it, and the reason was the toolchain, not the design.
\item \textbf{Nobody ran two engines against each other.} Six implementations of one specification exist and were tested only against their own oracles. The parallel synthesis has since compared two of them on a common fragment (Section~\ref{sec:parallel}).
\item \textbf{Nobody exhibited a nontrivial coverage proof.} The frame theorem's hypothesis is where its cost lives, and every executed instance discharges it by inspection.
\item \textbf{Nobody wrote the Lean frontier checker.} \Ju{} proposes it; the certificate theorem specifies it; it would have been the first kernel-checked statement about the round's own calculus. Its mathematical core is small, and its representation, registration and target bridges are where the work is.
\item \textbf{Everybody reused the same neighbours.} The four Frey mutations, the point-equality derivative, the \code{List Empty} witness and the $\Q[X]$ obstruction appear in eight or nine reports each. They were kernel-checked in round three. The new neighbours (\Fe's zero factor, \So's Dirac mass two, \Ju's discontinuous inverse branch, \Br's non-strict subgraph) are the ones worth adding to a suite, and each appears once.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Corrections to the corpus}\label{sec:corpus-correction}
Three corrections, two to the round-3 syntheses and one to this round.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The round-3 syntheses cite Leant revision \code{823259f7} as if it were a public revision. It is a local, unpushed commit (Section~\ref{sec:leant}). The descriptions are accurate; the citation was not reproducible, and four reports correctly refused to treat it as verified.
\item The round-3 review's \code{FreyExact.lean} states the first-numerator divisibility with the hypothesis $4\le p$. \Cl, \Ju, \La{} and \Ro{} observe that $2\le p$ suffices; \code{FreyRoutes.lean} proves it and exhibits $(3,2,3)$ as a witness that the sharpened interface is strictly larger. The round-3 statement was a sufficient condition, as it said; the per-coefficient interface the reports ask for should use the sharper one.
\item Several reports (\Al, \Cl, \He, \So) cite the online Lean reference as identifying version 4.34.0-rc2 or the 4.33.0 release notes as renaming \code{mvcgen'} to \code{vcgen}. This review did not access the network and cannot confirm either; the ProveIt pin is 4.32.0, and every report correctly keeps its execution claims at that pin.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What the convergence does and does not show}
Nine reports written independently against the same inputs converged on one calculus, six proofs of its main theorem, and one completeness criterion. That shows the specification is determined by the round-3 consensus plus the twenty questions; it does not show the specification is useful, since usefulness is the L3-versus-L2 comparison that no report can run. The nine engines show the calculus is implementable in a few hundred lines of Python; the two files of Sections~\ref{sec:routes} and~\ref{sec:frames-lean} show the two mathematical bridges the round needs are short in Lean. What the convergence cannot show is the thing every report names as the open question: whether an elaborator that reconstructs routine evidence at use sites reduces a mathematician's work beyond an equally equipped Lean library.

\subsection{The options ladder for the kernel question, unchanged}\label{sec:ladder}
All nine reports restate the round-3 ladder and add nothing to it that this review needs to consolidate: a library and renderer; an elaborator extension with property-implicit binders and residuals; a primitive refinement former with explicit evidence, translated to \code{Subtype}, gated on a measured encoding cost (\Fe{} proves its conservativity for the explicit fragment); restricted definitional coercion laws as a separate metatheoretic study (\Al{} and \Cl{} cite the definitional-functoriality work); and no open-ended semantic conversion. Two refinements are worth recording. \Al, \Br, \Cl, \He, \La{} and \Ro{} take care to say that the objection to semantic subtyping is \emph{not} the slogan that a timeout means false, since a sound implementation returns unknown; the objection is that conversion would inherit a search policy. And every report notes that proof erasure is already Lean's behaviour, so a primitive cannot be justified by runtime storage.

\subsection{A first slice ordered by what compiled}\label{sec:slice}
The round-3 review ordered a first slice by what compiled then; the order changes by what compiled now.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Start from \code{FreyRoutes.lean}.} It is a compiled exact-quotient use site with two alternative routes and a satisfiable fixture. Replace its hand-written instantiation by a registry: a validated entry for each of the three theorems, a term pool from the goal, and the route engine of any of the nine companions ported to Lean or driven from Lean. The gate is that the ported engine emits the same two routes and the same instantiation, and that S4, S22, S27 and S48 fail at the right boundary.
\item \textbf{Mechanize the frontier checker.} \Ju's certificate theorem over finsets; the nine companions' JSON certificates as its inputs; the cross-engine test of Section~\ref{sec:companions} as its regression.
\item \textbf{Extend \code{ListImage.lean} to a bridge.} Add \He's grammar interpretation lemma (already an ordinary induction) and an exact-candidate correspondence for one Leant provider term; then the diagonal and empty cases are end-to-end.
\item \textbf{Only then, locality.} The autonomous-derivative recipe with the quantified induction motive, against the same helper in ordinary Lean; S1 and S2 as the gate.
\item \textbf{The reading test before any syntax.} Twenty compact statements drawn from this round's examples, including \Fe's zero factor, \So's Dirac mass and \Ju's branch.
\end{enumerate}

First: do not prove Theorem~\ref{thm:antichain} again, and do not ship another generic-implication exporter; six proofs and thirteen compiled exports exist. Second: ship one Lean file that does something no report has done, and name it in the abstract. Candidates are listed in Section~\ref{sec:q5}.

\section{The parallel synthesis}\label{sec:parallel}
A second synthesis of the same nine reports, \emph{From Proof Obligations to Checkable Plans}, was written in parallel by Codex under \code{docs/round-4/synthesis/} and, after this review's first version reached \code{main}, revised to incorporate it. This section records the reconciliation. Its review memos, adversarial probes, compiler receipts and follow-up Lean files were read in full, and its seven follow-up Lean files were recompiled here (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}).

\subsection{Agreement}
The two syntheses agree on the round's object and both theorems, on the six-antichain versus three-closure split (it states the split more carefully than this review's first version did), on the same nineteen-of-twenty compiler result with the same Heather failure and the same Clover \code{olean} requirement, on every companion count, on the strict-seal versus role-classification fork, on the Frey sharpening to $p\ge2$ (which it had already checked in its own \code{FreySharper.lean}, including the cast identity under the sharper bound), and on the recommendation: one Lean use site completed end to end, a mechanized coverage checker, a list-length bridge with proved domains, and matched arms with a renderer control. Its ten questions for the next iteration and this review's ten overlap in eight.

\subsection{Corrections accepted}
The parallel synthesis's three memos on this report list twenty-odd corrections. The following were accepted and are applied above.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \code{ListImage.lean} has four theorems, not five, and they are not axiom-free. Its appended axiom audit was rerun here with identical results (Table~\ref{tab:new}).
\item Thirteen files are generic exports, not fourteen: twelve with the 22 generic theorems, plus \La's theorem-free cycle file. \Cl's core is concrete.
\item \code{FreyRoutes.lean} proves three cast identities and one conjunction of divisibilities, not four coefficient identities; ``unconditional'' there means that the generic rule premises are supplied, not that the arithmetic hypotheses vanish.
\item Five reports prove the general frame theorem (matrix row I2), not six; the prose now matches the matrix.
\item The six non-unanimous question rows are T1, T2, T5, T9, T10 and Q9, not six T-questions.
\item An affine hull of dimension $r$ needs $r+1$ points; a guard can lower the dimension or leave it unchanged; the tensor theorem's finite free factors are hypotheses, not a necessity; a seeded cycle yields a closed result when the seed is known and a conditional one when it is offered; an unfinished step may be indexed at its conditional type.
\item ``Nine implementations of one specification'', ``the calculus is finished'', ``the checker is a hundred lines'', ``all nine export generic Lean'', ``no report claimed more than it had'', and the stopping rule by unanimous rows were overstatements and are withdrawn or reworded.
\item The Leant commit is the head of a local branch ahead of its tracking ref; that it was not pushed from this checkout does not show it exists on no remote.
\item In the suite, \So's executed mark on S26 (reopening a used versus unused premise) and \Ro's on S27 (policy after minimisation) were analogies, not executions of the named operation; they are now P. An executed mark on a broad family does not certify every variant in it.
\end{enumerate}
Two of its corrections were declined. Its memo reads this review's S11 response ``recompute the frame'' as ``assume it shrinks''; the response is kept with the qualification now stated in Section~\ref{sec:completeness}. And its objection that the 101-row matrix is editorial coding rather than measurement is accepted as a description and was already the matrix's stated status (Section~\ref{sec:convergence}); the counts are retained as navigation.

\subsection{What it adds, and is adopted here}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Adversarial probes on the composed system.} Three \Cl{} inputs whose claim names are a Lean keyword or shadow the opened core's \code{compose} and \code{inj_comp}: the Python plan checker accepts them and Lean rejects the exports (rerun here: all three fail as reported). A \So{} input with rules $A\Rightarrow G$ and $B\wedge C\Rightarrow G$ and $B,C$ available: the model correctly reports an empty residual, but its command-line export selects the smaller total support $\{A\}$ and emits a valid conditional theorem where a closed proof was available. A \Ro{} input with $A\Rightarrow A$ and $A\wedge B\Rightarrow G$: the diagnostic frontier reports $B$, and supplying $B$ leaves the goal open, because the leaf frontier is an explanation, not a sufficient contract. A \Cl{} required root rule satisfied by reflexive equality and the already assumed goal. A stale output directory in which old positive \code{.lean} files survive a later failed request. These are new suite families S53--S57 (Appendix~\ref{app:suite}).
\item \textbf{A correction to Sorrel.} Its sentence that a bounded run retains a subset of the final label families is overstrong: a displayed support $\{B\}$ can later be dominated by $\varnothing$. The right monotone object is the upward closure, as \La{} says; a budgeted result should say ``sound routes, inventory incomplete, minimality not certified''.
\item \textbf{A cross-engine experiment.} \Fe{} and \Ju{} restricted to their common proposition-indexed fragment agree on 128 profiles and 768 target frontiers, with 89 Fennel certificates and all 128 Juniper results independently checked. This is the first evidence on T10 beyond each engine's own oracle, and U1 above now asks to extend it.
\item \textbf{The cast identity under the sharper bound.} \code{FreySharper.lean} proves \code{cast_a2_sharper} with $p\ge2$; this review's file proves only the divisibility under that bound. Rerun here: compiles, axioms as reported.
\item \textbf{A static Leant finding.} At the \code{823259f7} baseline, two tactic-suggestion paths display replacement text after a goal-count decrease without replaying the final spelling (\code{src/Main.hs} lines 5197--5211 and 5265--5270); exact replay of the displayed edit is a requirement, not a feature of that pin. This review did not verify it and records it as the parallel synthesis's source finding.
\item \textbf{Request-relative certificates and domain-certificate reuse.} Every response should be indexed by the request and context it answers; a coverage proof for $D$ restricts to $D'\subseteq D$ as a sound positive cover, while realizers and completeness may not. Both are adopted into Section~\ref{sec:slice}'s first gate and Section~\ref{sec:completeness}.
\item \textbf{A stricter reading of the suite tokens.} E, T and P measure different things and are not a strength ladder; a first slice should split each adopted family into an exact input, an expected status, and a named checker.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Where the two still differ}
The parallel synthesis presents the antichain engines and the route engines as components to combine; this review presents the antichain as the specification and the route engine as its first implementation. The difference is emphasis, and both now say that a route engine loses inventory and coverage. It keeps the Leant \code{823259f7} baseline as its inspected revision and treats publication as a separate question; this review keeps the reports' \code{3a40904b} as the citable revision and records the local branch state. It declines this review's suggestion that role-based sealing is ``the eventual design''; this review has reworded that to a fork to be tested (U7). Neither synthesis measured anything on a real Lean file, ran a reading test, or held out a task, and both say so.

\section{Recommendation}\label{sec:recommendation}
The recommendation is the round-3 recommendation with its first step already taken. Build one Lean slice, not a fifth design round. The slice is the exact-quotient use site of \code{FreyRoutes.lean} driven by a registry and a route engine, with the nine companions as test oracles and the 52-family suite as its gate; then the frontier checker; then the list-length bridge. Compare each against ordinary Lean supplied with the same theorems. Nothing in the nine reports would change that plan, and everything in them would be consumed by it.

If the campaign nevertheless runs a fifth round of reports, it should assign disjoint deliverables: one report ports one engine to Lean and reports T1 and T2 on \code{FreyRoutes.lean}; one mechanizes the frontier checker; one proves a nontrivial coverage theorem; one runs the reading test; one runs two companions against each other's fixtures. Nine more proofs of one theorem would be the first round of this campaign to add nothing.

\section{Questions for the fifth iteration}\label{sec:q5}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{U\arabic*.},itemsep=.5em]
\item \textbf{Do the six antichain engines agree?} Extend the parallel synthesis's Fennel--Juniper comparison to all six on a normalized Horn interchange format, with adapters that align known facts, offered atoms, nullary evidence, policies and completeness flags; compare the three closure engines only on projections (reachable facts, individually checked routes). A disagreement is a bug, an underdetermined specification, or an intended semantic difference; Theorem~\ref{thm:antichain} says which for the six.
\item \textbf{What does the registry cost on \code{FreyRoutes.lean}?} Replace the hand instantiation by validated entries and a term pool, and report instances generated, rules fired, proof-term size and elaboration time. This is T1 and T2 on a file that exists.
\item \textbf{What does a mechanized frontier checker cost?} Formalize \Ju's certificate theorem over a finite-profile datatype, bind the target, atom interpretation, known and offered sets, rules and policy, and check translated companion certificates with it; report where the effort went.
\item \textbf{Where is a coverage proof hard?} Exhibit a guarded input domain whose frame coverage needs more than inspection, and prove it.
\item \textbf{Can a route be exported from Lean, not to it?} The exporters go from Python to Lean. The first slice needs the reverse: a Lean goal, a term pool, a registry, and an emitted frontier.
\item \textbf{Which neighbours are new?} Kernel-check the new negative fixtures (\Fe's zero factor at $n=1$, $a=1$; \So's Dirac mass two; \Ju's discontinuous inverse branch; \Br's non-strict subgraph) as the round-3 review did for the Frey four.
\item \textbf{Does the strict sealing rule reject anything a mathematician writes?} \Fe{} and \Ro{} seal a metavariable-free target; collect the cases in the ProveIt corpus where a target legitimately contains a data hole.
\item \textbf{What does a reader recover?} Asked for the fourth time; run it on this round's twenty compact statements.
\item \textbf{Is the antichain ever needed interactively?} Instrument a route engine and count how often a second route would have changed the author's action.
\item \textbf{What ends this campaign?} Q10 asked when to end the separate-language experiment; the same question applies to the design rounds. The parallel synthesis is right that counting unanimous rows would reward reclassification, so the rule should be about delivered contracts: a round that reduces no uncertainty, produces no working artefact or counterexample, and measures nothing is the last.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
The fourth round did what a fourth design round can do, and it did it nine times. It took the round-3 object, a term whose proved properties accumulate without changing its identity, and gave its unfinished steps a semantics: a conditional term over a residual telescope, with the alternative ways to finish it computed as an antichain of minimal supports in a finite fragment whose grounding is bounded and whose completeness is relative and flagged. It took the round-3 correction about \code{List Empty} and turned it into a theorem: finite evidence decides an affine behavioural law exactly when a realizable frame covers the admissible image. Five or six reports prove each theorem; six engines compute the antichain and three compute routes; this review compiled every file they shipped, reproduced every count they reported, checked four facts behind the frames in core Lean, and instantiated their generic routes with the round-3 arithmetic so that three conditional routes became cast identities and a fourth a divisibility conjunction. A parallel synthesis reached the same compiler results, corrected this review's counts, and added the probes that show where the composed system, as opposed to its finite theorem, still fails.

The round did not produce an elaborator, a measurement, or a held-out task, and every report says so in its first paragraph. What it produced is a specification precise enough that nine independent implementations agree with independently written oracles, and two compiled bridges that show the mathematical content of the first slice is small. The next artefact is the slice. The campaign has now specified it four times; it should build it once.
