# Design review of the secondary round-two synthesis

Read-only review, 2026-09-05. I read all 768 lines of `docs/round-2/unified_report/unified_report.tex`, including appendices, and compared its semantic and design recommendations with `docs/round-2/synthesis/unified-report.tex`. Source line references below are one-based. References to **ours** identify the pre-incorporation report at merge commit `84af03f49f89a31b47ff3416ccdbc9180fd6b438`; the parent was revising that file concurrently, so its current working-tree line numbers will differ. Secondary-report and original-proposal references identify the source read at this merge. I did not edit either report, run Lean, inspect current external repositories, or audit the secondary report's feature counts or toolchain benchmarks. Those are separate review lanes. This memo's recommendations concern design evidence, not new implementation results.

Abbreviations: **secondary** = `docs/round-2/unified_report/unified_report.tex`; **ours** = `docs/round-2/synthesis/unified-report.tex`. The secondary report's numbered sections run through §12, followed by Appendices A–D.

## High-value incorporations

1. **Require a concrete accounting of what the proposed workspace adds to Lean.** Secondary §8.2, lines 491–494, and question R3 in §11, line 617, identify an omission in our report as well: a request/context/registry architecture should be compared directly with Lean's existing `section`, `variable`, local instances, local hypotheses, and simplification sets. Our §§2.2 and 4, lines 233–256 and 407–449, explain the semantic requirements but do not make this implementation comparison. Add a stage-one deliverable mapping each proposed field to either existing Lean state or genuinely additional retained metadata. Treat the Lean environment and local context as the typing authority; derive fact indexes, source mappings, route selections, guard provenance, and publication receipts from checked objects where possible. Then measure the added indexing and repair costs. This makes the library-first recommendation more concrete without assuming that existing Lean mechanisms already solve the display, provenance, or asynchronous scheduling problems.

2. **Make partial versus total operations an explicit initial policy decision.** Secondary §6.1, line 377, and R4, line 618, sharpen a difference that ours §§4.2 and 6, lines 474–480 and 604–643, describes mathematically but does not resolve operationally. The informative neighboring tasks are solving `(x²−1)/(x−1)=0` over the reals: total division gives solutions `{-1,1}`, whereas the partial expression with domain `x≠1` gives `{-1}`. Recommend preserving the already elaborated semantics when importing Lean and making a partial-expression package explicit in new requests. Compare both views in the reader experiment instead of silently making familiar CAS syntax change Lean's operations. This is a choice of default interpretation and migration policy, not an argument that one underlying logic is sounder.

3. **Measure Leant on the structural obligations the first CAS slices actually generate.** Secondary §8.3, line 509, and R8, line 622, ask a more specific question than our provider acceptance discussion. Our §9, lines 903–982, establishes the correct boundary; Appendix B/U8, lines 1329–1331, asks about adapter coverage and cost, but neither names a CAS-derived test population. Add a bounded experiment that records obligations produced by guarded cancellation, complete linear solving, and residual-to-jet assembly, then separates direct theorem lookup, deterministic theorem application, and multi-step structural composition. Give Leant and ordinary Lean retrieval exactly the same wrappers and budget. Its useful outcome is incremental solved obligations and reduced author work after all replay costs, not a generic synthesis success count. Qualify the secondary criticism: Noema already explicitly proposes measuring composition separately from single-lemma retrieval (§12.4, noema.tex 846–849; Appendix A.2, 905), so the missing part is concrete obligation selection, not an absence of evaluation ideas.

4. **Make the first verified checker a gate before broadening the system.** Secondary §9.4, lines 592–597, and §10, lines 605–607, give the useful ordering: one proved checker, its meaning theorem, and its original-target reification bridge before a second checker or broad grammar. Our §11.1, lines 1081–1093, already requires these pieces and an end-to-end exit gate. A short explicit priority sentence is enough; do not add another implementation architecture. Retain our stricter distinction between a finite arithmetic test and proof of the general checker theorem. The benchmark lane must separately assess the secondary report's claims about how much implementation work or checking cost remains.

5. **Add a cyclic-guard mutation to the initial negative suite.** Secondary §6.2, line 402, and Appendix B/M43, line 710, preserve a sharp Noema example missing from our displayed suite (§11.5, lines 1155–1181). The genuine source is Noema §5.2, noema.tex 289–294. A proposed result `G → P` may be retained, but the system must not insert `P` into a fact index and then use it to establish `G`. A matched positive case supplies `G` independently and then applies the conditional result. The kernel already prevents an actual circular proof term; this test detects a frontend, scheduler, or display that prematurely treats a conditional claim as an available unconditional fact. It is more discriminating than another ordinary denominator failure.

6. **Keep genuinely new examples separate from demonstrations and controls.** Secondary §9.5, lines 600–601, proposes an uncited-file requirement. Our §11.3, lines 1109–1116, already requires a previously unseen theorem and explicitly says a case used to design the system is not held out. Retain our rule, adding deliberate diversity of mathematical structures or domains if useful. An uncited filename alone does not prevent near-duplicate tasks or dependency leakage. Conversely, the secondary proposal to use Bell and Abel as held-out tests (§9.3, line 587; R5, line 619) is invalid for a system designed around the reports' Bell and Abel examples. Those are demonstrations or controls; select fresh related theorems before implementation and prevent target aliases or downstream consequences from entering retrieval. Vantage §15.3, vantage.tex 1989–1994, gives the more exact leakage rule.

7. **Use a small merge/upgrade experiment to test retained evidence, rather than designing a collaboration system now.** Secondary §8.3, lines 507–508, and R7, line 621, raise meaningful maintenance cases. Our §11.3, lines 1118–1123, already includes controlled dependency upgrades but not concurrent author edits. Add two source branches that independently change a route/policy or a witness-bound scope, merge them, and check which retained receipts remain valid in the merged context. For toolchain migration, record the old and new validator/toolchain and axiom policy, revalidate exact targets, and issue new receipts; an old axiom-name pattern must not silently become the new policy. This is an additional concrete mutation, not evidence that the proposals ignored versioning altogether: Prism §16.3, prism.tex 958–963, explicitly denies a cross-version promise for `.olean` or internal expression formats, and Noema §10.4, noema.tex 756–761, requires new evidence in the new environment.

## Do not import the promotion table literally

The secondary §9.1 table (lines 518–540) is a useful prompt to turn result relationships into explicit theorem interfaces, but several rows are not correct contracts. Ours §§4.2 and 10.1, lines 451–480 and 1014–1034, already give the safer formulation: particular guarantee predicates and checked entailments with their hypotheses, without a universal rank of result kinds.

| Secondary locator | Problem | Correct formulation / negative neighbor |
|---|---|---|
| §9.1, line 529 | A witness `P(w)` plus coverage `∀x, P(x) → x∈S` does not make `S` a complete solution set. | Also require soundness `∀x∈S, P(x)`, yielding `∀x, P(x) ↔ x∈S`. Counterexample: `P(x)` is `x=0`, `w=0`, and `S={0,1}`. The displayed premises hold but `S` contains an invalid root. Prism §4.2, prism.tex 258 and 264, states the correct two directions. |
| §9.1, line 533 | Equality at a point plus openness does not imply neighborhood equality. | Require equality throughout an open set containing the point, or an explicit eventual-equality theorem. `f(x)=x` and `g(x)=0` on the open set `R` agree at zero but not near it. Ours §8.3, lines 865–882, already keeps these premises distinct. |
| §9.1, line 536; feature B5, line 259 | An enclosure can imply equality; “Never” and “an interval containing zero decides nothing” are too strong. | If `l=u`, antisymmetry gives `x=l`; in particular `[0,0]` proves `x=0`. Bounds containing zero can also prove a non-strict sign. A nondegenerate enclosure alone does not identify its midpoint as the value or determine a strict sign when zero remains possible. Appropriate bounds for every precision can yield equality through a further theorem. |
| §9.1, line 537 | Unit derivative alone is not the full residual-to-jet bridge. | Retain an existing root, its defining equation, the common constant coefficient/branch, and the specified precision, as in secondary §6.2 line 390 and our Proposition 1 in §7.1, lines 684–698. For `F(Y)=Y²−1` over `Q`, `J=−1` has exact zero residual and unit derivative at `−1`, but is not the jet of the separately selected root `+1`. |
| §9.1, line 528 | Calling calculus nonderivability a “weaker claim” on an upward ladder invites the exact error the report elsewhere rejects. | A theorem `P→A` plus an actual checked proof of `¬A` gives `¬P`; absence of a derivation of `A` is not that proof. Preserve our §9.2's original-target distinction and the secondary report's own better explanation in §3.2, line 220. |

If incorporating a new table, call it examples of **additional evidence required for a particular inference**, with explicit input and output predicates. Do not imply that the rows define a total ladder or that table membership itself supplies a proof.

## Other conclusions requiring qualification

- **An extensible specification interface is the consensus; a closed result-kind universe is not established.** Secondary §§1.3, 4.3, and 8.1, lines 157, 320, and 481, repeatedly describe a small fixed or closed family. Its own §3.1, lines 213–217, uses a general dependent output/specification interface. Prism §4.1, prism.tex 235–246, likewise allows an arbitrary request predicate `Claim_q : B_q → Prop`. Keep a deliberately small initial vocabulary for UI and implementation, but permit domain packages to introduce propositions and certified bridges without changing a foundational enumeration. This directly supports our Q2, lines 1208–1210, as an initial vocabulary decision rather than a closed semantic universe.

- **Compatible logical cores do not make workspace, transaction, presentation, and query abstractions merely different spellings.** Secondary §§1.3 and 3.1, lines 157 and 211–217, flatten them too far. The common dependent-pair interface says what acceptance means; it does not determine where persistent state lives, when computation is requested, how representation choices are made, or how authors repair a document. Preserve our separate discussion of implementation and surface choices (§10, lines 984–1012). Consensus can close the repeated kernel debate without settling those workflow questions or proving there is only one sensible design point (§8.4, secondary line 513).

- **Keep evidence mechanism and execution trust as independent axes.** Secondary C1 (§4.1, line 264) uses “two lanes” for a verified algorithm versus an untrusted producer with a verified checker. Its conclusion (§12, line 628) uses similar language for strict kernel checking versus accelerated execution with extra assumptions. These are different distinctions. Either an algorithm or a checker can have a justified strict evaluation route; both also face the native execution gap. Ours §5.1, lines 528–554, plus §5.2, lines 559–576, should remain the organizing account.

- **Many encodings of one object do not require a relational rather than functional decoder.** Secondary §6.1, line 379, motivates relations partly through “many-to-one representations.” A decoding function can already map many valid representations to the same object. A genuinely lossy observation, or representation data compatible with several different objects, needs an observation relation or a distinct abstract semantic object. Our exact-presentation/restricted-observation/directed-abstraction distinction (§4.1, lines 430–449) is more precise and already incorporates Vantage's contribution.

- **Do not adopt blanket absence claims about upgrade handling or Leant evaluation.** Secondary §8.3, lines 507–509, and R7–R8, lines 621–622, identify useful experiments, but existing source passages cited above already discuss new receipts across versions and composition-specific evaluation. The accurate conclusion is that these plans need an executed, precisely scoped test, not that the reports supply no design answer.

- **The promotion table, residual theorem, retained evidence, and finite-jet distinction largely reinforce work already present in ours.** The secondary recommendations do not justify replacing our staged plan. The highest-value changes are the workspace-to-Lean accounting deliverable, the partial/total default decision, a CAS-derived Leant experiment, and the cyclic-guard negative test. Keep the end-to-end soundness gate, independent reading study, equally equipped four-arm comparison, and genuine holdout rule already in our report.
