## Findings worth carrying into the synthesis

All three locate the main opportunity in a property-aware elaborator above ordinary Lean, retaining mathematical data while accumulating scoped proofs. They distinguish semantic structure choices, proof-only laws, and delegated witness construction. Their shared contribution is making these distinctions operational through theorem-backed demands, rather than increasing foundational expressive power. Their proposed metatheorems are written relative translation arguments for small explicit rules, not verified compiler results.

The main differences are useful engineering choices:

| Decision | Schist | Tephra | Trellis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Default inference | Backward sufficient requirement transformers, cheap facts indexed | Deterministic cheap forward consequences plus bounded backward search | Finite grounded vocabulary and full closure in a backward-closed relevant subgraph |
| First arithmetic checker | Natural affine equality in one variable | Sparse integer-polynomial positive ideal certificates | Rational affine nonnegativity certificates over real valuations |
| Distinct mathematical development | Probability uniqueness through characteristic profiles and orbital comparison | Prefix-local inverse reversal; restriction of a chosen action to torsion | Sharp Lipschitz constants; quotient derivatives; vanishing errors; exact Frey coefficient casts |
| Leant-specific extension | Split proof, specified-data, and exact-edit requests | Proved provider laws, realizable counterexamples, explicit uniformity | Behavioral law registry and matched composition/direct-lemma evaluation |
| Inference guarantee | Sufficient demand composition, no completeness promise | Bounded strategy, no strongest refinement | Least closure relative to fixed finite rules; not global mathematical completeness |

This is not a conflict requiring one winning brand. A bounded backward phase can choose a finite relevant rule graph, then Trellis's closure can deterministically fill it. Schist's transformer contracts provide a natural registration interface for edges. Preserve the limits: finite closure says nothing about the cost/completeness of generating that graph, finding useful theorem instances, or selecting among incomparable sufficient routes.

## Schist: valuable points and exact qualifications

**Demand-driven method contracts.** `schist/schist.tex:639` ("A method tells the elaborator what it needs") specifies `ReqSound(f, req)`: `req Q a -> Q(f a)`. Composition is `req_f (req_g R)`, justified by two applications; no weakest-condition claim or monotonicity assumption is needed for this soundness fact. The relational generalization at `:676` retains ordered premises and intermediate data. The default at `:695` is backward search with cheap registered facts, avoiding expensive saturation of every restricted domain and every product fact. Residual diagnostics at `:712` say what a selected method needs, not what is mathematically necessary for the theorem.

**A nonalgebraic test of generality.** `schist/schist.tex:824`–`:970`, especially "A general orbital-comparison theorem" at `:886`, supplies the most distinctive mathematical case. If `T^n x -> p`, `f,g` are continuous at `p`, have the same value there, and share recurrences `f(x)=a(x)f(Tx)`, `g(x)=a(x)g(Tx)` with `|a(x)|<=1`, then `f=g`. Indeed `d(x)<=d(T^n x)->0`. No continuity of `T` is required once orbit convergence is given; only continuity at `p` is used. This yields uniqueness of contractive affine independent-copy probability laws through characteristic functions without a moment assumption, cancellation of a possibly vanishing multiplier, or an existence conclusion. The optional bound on products `|product_{j<n} a(T^j x)|<=M_x` weakens the pointwise multiplier bound. These written proofs are sound as stated, but are library-level abstractions; they do not prove that a new frontend is necessary. Paired controls are `q=1` (operator can be the identity), unequal normalization, and adding a finite-moment hypothesis to an originally unrestricted probability theorem.

**Type and scope choices.** `schist/schist.tex:440` (local open rows), `:601` (allowed-assumption masks/context maps), `:620` (data cannot be allocated invisibly), and `:1078` (lexical structure environment) form a coherent conservative interface. Whole-function laws at `:760` supplement pointwise contracts; independent random variables, equal marginals, and equal laws are not flattened into unary adjectives. The full sorting predicate at `:729` correctly requires permutation and sortedness, not length plus sortedness.

**Strong evaluation decomposition.** `schist/schist.tex:1626` gives L0 ordinary Lean; L1 same new mathematical packages; L2 same services; L3 property typing; L4 narrative. L3/L2 isolates the key claim. Infrastructure is charged by `I + sum(A_j+M_j)` at `:1648`, and stopping rules at `:1700` permit shipping only the useful libraries/services. This is more informative than visible token comparisons or a generic "Lean baseline."

**Evidence limits.** `SchistCore.lean:98` writes `eval_nf`; `:111` writes `check_sound`; `:125` reconstructs `3*(x+2)=3*x+6`; `:128` rejects constant 7. This is the most complete small Lean checker candidate in this lane, but the authors did not compile it. The source imports `Lean`, uses explicit induction and `decide`, and contains eight `#print axioms` queries. Recommend the root compile it serially and retain the exact outcome; do not infer compilation from readable proof scripts. `contract_compose` at `:40` is a stronger-premise/simplified variant of full two-stage contracts, while `composeObservations` at `:52` preserves a genuinely dependent intermediate witness. The Python companion checks 50 affine fixtures at 13 points, plus concrete negative neighbors; its constructor is not a hardened parser (for example it checks nonnegativity without rejecting all non-integer Python inputs). The authors explicitly advertise finite fixtures, so this is an implementation boundary rather than a contradiction of their claim.

## Tephra: valuable points and exact qualifications

**Observation realizability is essential for hard rejection.** `tephra/tephra.tex:909` ("A missing behavioral premise: observation realizability") proves

`(exists xs : List A, length xs = n) <-> (n = 0 or Nonempty A)`.

Therefore the constant-empty function on `List Empty` preserves length: only the empty list exists. An abstract length witness `n=1` cannot refute it. When the element type is universally quantified, an authorized instantiation with an inhabited type may realize a witness; when it is fixed, the checker cannot substitute another type. A propositional nonemptiness proof can suffice to refute a proposition, while an executable witness generator needs actual data or explicit choice. This is a particularly valuable next test for the Leant adapter; it is a mathematical warning about stronger source-level authority, not an allegation that the current Leant boundary claims that authority.

The more general direction distinction at `:892` is correct: absence of abstract violations can establish absence of source violations given `Bad_src -> Bad_abs`; an abstract violation needs realization/reflection before it refutes a concrete candidate. Even a realized counterexample refutes that candidate's universal specification, not existence of another successful implementation. The passive-law versus proof-linked-law alternatives at `:880` allow incremental integration: certify every transfer theorem and interpreter soundness, or use the abstraction only to suggest a candidate/witness that Lean checks directly.

**Polymorphism is not parametricity.** `tephra/tephra.tex:935` proposes explicit uniformity/naturality contracts instead of free theorems for arbitrary Lean inhabitants. Independently verified against the official [Lean Axioms reference](https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/latest/Axioms/), §8.2, accessed 2026-09-06: it supplies a noncomputable type-sensitive list function and demonstrates incompatibility of blanket parametricity with Lean's standard axioms. The report's restriction to a proved relational translation of an admitted source fragment is the appropriate later research boundary. Do not simplify it into a keyword blacklist on `classical`.

**Observation coverage and behavioral locality.** The commuting-square contract at `tephra/tephra.tex:440` is `o_B . f = fhat . o_A`; joint faithfulness at `:459` is a separate all-observations theorem. "Reversing a prefix-local linear inverse" (`:493`–`:598`) uses this accurately. For linear prefix-local endomorphisms of `R^N` streams over a commutative ring, every finite restriction is a square matrix, so a one-sided inverse reverses; equality of every finite prefix gives global equality. The determinant/adjugate proof is sound and needs no field division. Shift maps show linearity alone is insufficient; coordinatewise doubling/halving over integer streams shows prefix locality alone is insufficient. The explicit warning at `:576` against pretending an arbitrary abelian group's finite power is finite free over Z preserves the original binomial-inversion generality. A broader synthesis could name the actual abstraction: jointly faithful observations into categories/endomorphism monoids where one-sided inverses reverse, with compatible restrictions. Such a generalization is not an implemented library result here.

**Action restriction keeps data identity.** `tephra/tephra.tex:601` proves restriction of a chosen monoid action to a preserved predicate. The induced action does not imply additive-subgroup closure. At `:621`, additive automorphisms preserve n-torsion, and commutativity makes that torsion set an additive subgroup. Dimension two, nontriviality, irreducibility, and absolute irreducibility are correctly kept separate at `:650`. This is a useful non-analytic test of inference and structure selection.

**Actual checker semantics, with the missing implementation named.** `tephra/tephra.tex:698`–`:780` gives sparse integer coefficients, evaluation in any commutative ring, operation-denotation lemmas and positive ideal-certificate soundness. The mathematical proofs are correct and do not need a domain. Mismatched multiplier/premise lists must fail before `zip`. Source-specific reification and the execution equation remain separate obligations. `TephraCore.lean` imports only `Init`, has six theorem declarations and several proof-bearing definitions; it deserves a focused compilation/axiom audit. It does not contain polynomial checker soundness, the prefix inverse theorem, the list-length-image theorem, or a complete action structure. Its `restrict_comp` merely proves the value-level compositional encoding, consistent with the README's narrow status.

**A wording point to improve in synthesis.** "Weakest registered sufficient input guarantee" (`tephra/tephra.tex:468`) should not become a promise that a unique weakest route exists. Two registered sufficient guards can be incomparable. A finite registry can provide minimal alternatives or a proved disjunction route; selecting a canonical least guard needs additional structure. Schist's plain "sufficient" is the safest shared terminology.

## Trellis: valuable points and exact qualifications

**A precise finite closure guarantee.** `trellis/trellis.tex:382` fixes finite well-typed ground qualifiers. "Ground" includes arbitrary local variables with already fixed Lean terms, not just numeral test inputs. At `:394`, closure terminates after at most `|Q\S0|` strict additions, retains an acyclic proof DAG, and computes a schedule-independent least rule-closed fact set. The proof is sound. `:418` restricts demand closure to a backward dependency subgraph that includes every premise of every retained rule. Heuristic pruning forfeits completeness relative to omitted rules. The linear bookkeeping bound excludes rule generation, theorem instantiation, checking and proof size. This is a useful specification of one inference component, not a measured elaborator performance claim or a principal type theorem for all Lean predicates. The Python companion does not implement this closure algorithm.

**Mathematical strength is itself a contract.** The sharp Fabius/Rvachev result (`trellis/trellis.tex:565`) distinguishes a `2`-Lipschitz upper bound from leastness. Derivative laws include existence, range laws supply exact affine certificates, and a derivative attaining magnitude 2 proves the lower bound on every Lipschitz constant. The generalization at `:612` is a useful ordinary Lean wrapper. A hypothetical totalized derivative expression without differentiability evidence would not suffice. Endpoints at `:603` preserve the `x>=0` condition when removing `abs x`.

**Analytic and algebraic work separate cleanly.** Quotient differentiation (`trellis/trellis.tex:618`) first obtains the quotient rule under `x!=0` and `g1(x)!=0`, then normalizes its numerator using definitions. I checked the displayed expansion: the discrepancy factors as `r1*(-g1+x*(g2-r2)+r1)=0`. The algebraic certificate cannot establish derivative existence or the guards. The vanishing-error lemma at `:663` is also sound: if `e(epsilon)->0` on a specified right interval and `X<=K+e(epsilon)` for all admissible epsilon, then `X<=K`. Its specialization `c*epsilon^2` works for any real c; c>=0 is unnecessary. This is a library generalization, not evidence of a frontend gain.

**Relational reflection and exact model transport.** At `trellis/trellis.tex:699`, positive powers preserve and reflect integer divisibility, yielding equality of normalized pairwise gcds under a Fermat-type equation. The proof correctly treats zero and signs. Counterexamples are exponent zero and the weaker `d | e^p` premise. At `:731`, the actual Frey residue conditions supply exact division by 4 and 16 before casting into Q. Similar printed fractions do not suffice. The mathematical arguments are sound as stated; final structure equality after base change still needs its extensionality theorem.

**A workload-matched small checker.** `trellis/trellis.tex:764` supplies rational nonnegative multipliers for affine rows over real valuations, and proves soundness by finite distributivity and order. There is no completeness claim: even positive constant 1 cannot be certified by an empty collection of hypothesis rows unless the format includes a trivial positive row/slack. The companion deliberately tests this rejection. The reifier at `:805` has a paper induction and exact atom anchoring requirement; Lean reification, checker proof and checked execution are future work. This is a better first fit to Fabius bounds than a new unrestricted polynomial engine.

**Three forms of dependency.** `trellis/trellis.tex:918` distinguishes a public theorem's required premise, a premise supplied to this application, and a dependency retained by the proof. This matters when a theorem accepts a formally unused premise. A faithful mathematical account should not infer indispensability from API shape or presence in a transitive dependency list. Required named-method fidelity also needs a genuine argument node, not a dummy theorem reference buried in an unrelated proof.

## Recommended next discussion questions

1. Should the first inference API consume ordinary Lean theorem binders with semantic role metadata, or require an explicit sufficient-requirement transformer? Can both compile into one small rule representation?
2. How is the finite qualifier universe generated, and what is the stopping rule when theorem instantiation introduces new terms? Relative closure completeness should never be confused with completeness of that generation step.
3. Which of the three first checkers serves a real initial workload at lowest total implementation cost? Existing `omega`, `linarith`, `linear_combination`, `ring` and `grobner` controls should receive the same wrappers.
4. Is Leant allowed to reject candidates using an abstract witness, and if so, where is the exact source-type realizability proof constructed? Include `List Empty`, fixed versus quantified type binders, and refinement-restricted inputs.
5. Which distinctions are mandatory in the ordinary mathematical display, and which need expansion? Test leastness versus an upper bound, law equality versus object equality, and local versus pointwise differentiation.
6. How should several incomparable sufficient routes be ranked and displayed without pretending that one is a weakest precondition or that search failure is mathematical necessity?
7. Can a small matched experiment separate library abstraction from inference benefit? Probability orbital comparison, prefix inverse reversal and least-Lipschitz wrappers must be equally available in ordinary Lean.
8. What evidence would justify a kernel experiment at all? Require a measured bottleneck in the conservative implementation plus a specific metatheory, rather than treating a foundational change as the default meaning of richer mathematical types.
